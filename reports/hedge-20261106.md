# Market Hedge While the Gate Is Closed, 2026-11-06

**Question:** four strategies carry the SPY-trend market gate (`market-gate-20261106.md`): no new longs while SPY
is at or below its N-day average. While the gate is closed the book sits mostly in cash and learns nothing from a
falling market. Would a small long in SH (ProShares Short S&P 500, the daily inverse of SPY), placed through the
ordinary long machinery, earn its keep?

**Decision rule (fixed before the run, in `scripts/run_hedge_backtest.py`):** a hedge size is eligible only if, in
every window (all, 2015+, 2020+), the book's total profit with the hedge is not lower than without it **and** the
book's max drawdown is lower. Among eligible sizes the smallest is adopted. None eligible = no hedge; the strategy
row is created paused.

**Hedge policy (one function, `strategies/market_hedge.hedge_state`, shared by the backtest and the live strategy):**
enter on the close of a SPY bar whose close is below its 50-day average and whose 10-day average is below the
50-day; fill at SH's next open. Exit at the next open after SPY closes back above its 50-day average (the same bar
that reopens the gate for a 50-day-gated long); a 10% catastrophe stop on SH; no target. Sizes 2%, 5% and 10% of
equity, marked to market daily. The hedge is one more long in the book: it takes a slot under the live caps and
the 10-session stop cooldown, so a refused entry is counted. Standard fills and costs (`triple_barrier`: next-open
entry, volatility-scaled slippage both sides, stop first, gaps at the open).

**Data.** 45 paper symbols, 2000-01-03 to 2026-11-05, the same cache as the gate, slot and regime reports; the
live gates (SPY market gates, Channel Breakout's VIX < 20, the 10-session earnings blackout). SH history starts
2006-06-21. Before that the hedge is a **short in SPY** built from SPY's own bars (every move inverted, a 50
bps/year borrow cost), chained into SH's first open so a trade straddling the splice sees no jump. 35 of the 134
hedge trades fall in the synthetic segment.

### The hedge leg on its own

| Window | Trades | Win rate | Net bps per trade [95% CI] | Sessions hedged | Mean hold | Stops |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| all | 134 | 16% | **−113.8** [−166.4, −53.5] | 28% | 13.3 | 0 |
| 2015+ | 59 | 14% | −151.1 [−197.7, −103.2] | 24% | 11.0 | 0 |
| 2020+ | 29 | 21% | −167.5 [−254.7, −77.1] | 23% | 12.6 | 0 |

This is the rare result here whose confidence interval clears zero: the leg loses. Costs are a quarter of it
(gross −88 bps, net −114 bps per trade). The catastrophe stop never fired; every exit was SPY reclaiming its
average.

**Why it loses.** The entry is a dip below a falling average; the exit is the first close back above it. That
pair pays only when the dip turns into a bear market and whipsaws the rest of the time:

* 63 of 134 trades lasted five sessions or fewer and averaged −183 bps. 34 lasted more than 20 sessions and
  averaged +151 bps.
* By year, the leg made money in 2001 (+8.0%), 2002 (+9.2%) and 2008 (+15.5%, the best trade: entered 2008-09-10,
  out 2008-12-17 for +16.9%), and lost in every other year, including 2020 (−22.4% at 10%, seven trades: the
  March crash was one quick win followed by six reclaims) and 2022 (−1.4%, six trades, the long slide kept
  crossing its own average).
* The long book is already light when the gate is closed, so there is less to hedge: the hedge's drawdown
  improvement is small because the losses it would offset mostly happen before the gate closes (the cluster
  stops the loss diagnosis found), not after.

A slower exit (SPY's 10-day average back above the 50-day, or a close above the 50-day held for N sessions) or
a later entry (N sessions below the average before buying) would cut the whipsaw, and so would a hedge ratio
tied to the book's open exposure rather than equity. None of those were in the pre-registered rule, so none is
adopted here; each is a separate experiment for the engine to run with its own rule fixed first.

## What ships

The strategy ships either way so the forward loop can run it on evidence, not on this report:

* `app/services/strategies/market_hedge.py`: `MarketHedgeStrategy` (`market_hedge`), long-only on SH through the
  normal paper long path, so every cap, protection, reservation and exit mechanism applies. Entry is the inverse
  of the market gate (`market_gate.spy_above_sma` on SPY, plus the 10-day average below the 50-day), exit is the
  policy's indicator exit when SPY reclaims the average, with a 10% broker-side catastrophe stop and no target.
  SPY comes from the trusted daily history the gate uses, cached per process; only SPY bars dated on or before
  the traded frame's last bar are consulted, and missing or stale SPY history fails closed. `hedge_fraction`
  (the adopted size, here none) is a row parameter reported in the signal features; the paper cycle still sizes
  every entry through its own risk rules, so an exploration candidate is learning-sized.
* **Fence.** Every other strategy is instantiated through `fenced_class`: a BUY on a hedge-only symbol (SH)
  becomes a gated HOLD (`gated_action`, `hedge_fence` in the features), so a trend rule firing on SH never
  becomes an order. `isinstance` checks and class names are unchanged.
* **Market gate.** The hedge row never carries `market_gate_sma` and `MARKET_GATED_STRATEGIES` does not list it,
  so the gate that closes the long book never blocks the hedge (tested against a falling-market gate state).
* **Caps.** `trading_universe.sector_group("SH")` is its own `inverse` group (it was falling back to a one-name
  group already), so the hedge never competes with index longs under the per-sector cap; one position per symbol
  and the total cap apply as to any long.
* **Universe.** SH joins the execution universe only through `settings.paper_hedge_symbols`
  (`DEFAULT_PAPER_HEDGE_SYMBOLS`, empty because nothing was adopted). Research scripts replay
  `DEFAULT_PAPER_EXECUTION_SYMBOLS` and never see it. Admitting the hedge later means `PAPER_HEDGE_SYMBOLS=SH`.
* **Admission.** `seed.admit_market_hedge` (audit action `hedge_admission`) creates the `Market Hedge` row once:
  `paper_trading_candidate` had a size been adopted, `paused` now. An operator who deletes, re-statuses or makes
  their own row is never overridden, and the row is never recreated.

Tests: `tests/test_market_hedge.py` (signals on synthetic SPY/SH, no lookahead and fail-closed, registry and
exit policy, the fence, the sector group and universe, the script's decision rule and synthetic short, the
seed's idempotency and operator-override contract, the admitted-vs-paused outcome).

## Not measured

* The live fill would be the daily-bar entry window's limit order, not the next open.
* SH's own costs (0.89% expense ratio, daily rebalancing drift) are in its real history from 2006 but not in the
  synthetic 2000–2006 segment beyond the borrow cost.
* The hedge was sized on equity. A hedge sized on the book's open long exposure (small when the gate is closed)
  would be smaller still, so it cannot rescue the result, but it is the natural next variant.

Artifacts: `scripts/run_hedge_backtest.py` (prints the table and writes the JSON; `--download` fetches SH with
the rest).
