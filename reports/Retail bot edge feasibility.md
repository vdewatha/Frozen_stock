# Trade less: the only lever arithmetic guarantees

Frozen_stock has no demonstrated edge in its current space, and more searching there will not produce one. Before any cost, its 1-20 session signals earn about what holding the same stocks, or SPY, over the same days would have earned, so the roughly **22 times equity a year in turnover** they spend buys no timing value. Replayed on today's 45 names at 5% of equity a trade and priced with the published cost model, the book compounded about **4% a year at a 17.5% maximum drawdown** over 2015-2026, while a static, monthly-rebalanced **50/50 SPY/T-bill mix earned 7.9% a year at a 17.6% drawdown**; at 5-10 bps a round trip that gap would roughly close, and survivor flattery of about the same size runs the other way, so its size is unsettled even though the missing timing value is not. The best of the six directions is therefore the first: move slower, inside a 4-8x annual turnover budget, as one netted target-weight book whose unassigned capital sits in a passive SPY/T-bill arm instead of cash, at an equity weight the owner sets as a risk budget. That closes the cost and idle-capital leaks rather than finding alpha, and the passive arm becomes the bar every other idea must clear. Inside that frame the plausible edges are small: mean reversion after sell-offs in the four index ETFs, the only family whose survivorship-free net expectancy since 2015 is meaningfully positive (IBS +22.0 and RSI(2) +13.7 bps a trade, against +6.4 bps for RSI(14) on the point-in-time universe, every interval including zero), though it fails the horizon note's full admission rule at the published cost model and is borderline at 10 bps a round trip; one pre-registered offline run of a pooled cross-sectional ranker on a 20-session label; and month-end rebalancing pressure as a timing overlay worth a few basis points. Cross-asset ETF trend is cheap to build and cuts drawdowns relative to SPY, but tranched and at the bot's own cost model it **lost to a volatility-matched static SPY/T-bill blend on both return and Sharpe after 2015**, and a slice of it funded from T-bills raised the passive arm's Sharpe over 2008-2026 but not since 2015, so it gets the machinery and a shadow record, not capital. Intraday effects and pairs are dropped. None of the Sharpe-judged sleeves can be verified forward within one to three years, and even the per-stock event logs need about two years for a t = 2 read before earnings-season clustering is counted, so adoption has to rest on pre-registered point-in-time replay with per-family trial accounting, and forward paper trading becomes an implementation check rather than a verdict.

## Seven failed searches share one cause: turnover spent buying drift

The bot's own replays show the problem is missing timing value, not bad luck or a missing feature. Sector-relative reversal is the best-documented surviving short-horizon effect in large caps. On the point-in-time S&P 500 since 2015 its default rule earned **+21.3 bps a trade gross** over 3.3-day holds, against 31.7 bps of modeled cost. Its net of −10.3 bps and its excess over the stock's own drift of −33.3 bps imply that holding the same stocks for the same number of days would have earned about +23 bps ([sector-reversal report](sector-reversal-20261107.md)). The core list's long-only signals trail SPY's move over the same days by **33.7 bps a trade** since 2015, net of the published model and a 10.1 bps extra-spread charge, so before costs they roughly match it, and every point-in-time liquidity tercile outside the core loses money per trade before any extra spread is charged ([liquid-universe report](liquid-universe-20261107.md)). Post-upgrade news drift made +169 to +186 bps a trade but could not beat holding the stock, with an excess-over-drift interval of [−90, +93] bps ([news-entries report](news-entries-20261106.md)). Today's 45-name list flatters these strategies by 20-70 bps a trade, and no broad technical strategy keeps an interval above zero once the universe is point in time ([survivorship report](survivorship-20261106.md)). The regime-allocation and sizing searches ended with intervals of [−4.0, +42.2] and [−3.1, +42.4] bps, which means "no evidence for" rather than "evidence against" ([regime-allocation report](regime-allocation-20261106.md); [position-sizing report](position-sizing-20261106.md)). The inverse-ETF hedge leg lost 151 bps a trade since 2015 ([hedge report](hedge-20261106.md)). Rule mutation found every candidate's deflated Sharpe at 0.0 at 382 trials, and the library now stands at 422 ([rule-mutation report](rule-mutation-20261106.md)).

Book-level arithmetic separates what the replay settles from what it does not. The settled part needs no cost assumption. On the core list since 2015 the long-only signals netted +17.5 bps a trade under a model charging roughly 30 bps, while SPY moved about +41 bps over the same days, so before costs they earned about what holding SPY would have earned (my arithmetic from the [liquid-universe report](liquid-universe-20261107.md)); sector reversal shows the same thing against each stock's own drift. The size of the leak is not settled. The 2015+ replayed book, which trades today's 45 names at **5% of equity a trade**, took 5,240 trades and finished up 60.0% with a 17.5% maximum drawdown ([hedge report](hedge-20261106.md)). That is about 4.0% a year, against 13.5% for SPY over nearly the same window ([ETF note](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/etf_rotation.md)). A static 50/50 SPY/T-bill mix rebalanced monthly earned **7.9% a year with a 17.6% drawdown** over 2015-2026 in my rerun on the note's cached prices, and the note's daily-rebalanced version shows 8.0% and −17.9% ([static-blend check](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/static_check.py); [output](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/static_check.out.txt)). The trade count is roughly 22 times equity in one-sided turnover a year, so the book's cost drag is about 22 times its round-trip cost: **5-7% of equity a year at the published model's 23-33 bps**, more than it kept, but only 1.1-2.2% at 5-10 bps. Re-priced at the step-1 bracket, the book would have earned roughly 8-10% a year at 5 bps a round trip, 7-9% at 10 bps, 4-6% at 25 bps and 4.0% at the model (my arithmetic from the hedge and cost reports), so at planning costs it roughly ties the passive arm. The replay errs the other way too. The seven broad technical strategies take about nine in ten of its trades ([position-sizing report](position-sizing-20261106.md)), and today's list flatters them by a median 33 bps a trade since 2015 ([survivorship report](survivorship-20261106.md)), roughly 6-7% a year at this turnover (my arithmetic); no point-in-time version of the book exists. Over the full 2000-2026 replay it made **+1.5% in total with a 56.6% drawdown**. The paper record points the same way. The largest measured loss in the first audit was idle capital, **$1,026.64 behind SPY in 46 days** with no autonomous orders ([paper audit](paper-results-audit-20261027.md)), and the live risk rule still sizes each scheduled BUY at 1% of equity ([position-sizing report](position-sizing-20261106.md)). At the published cost model, turnover and time out of the market are the leaks, not signal choice; at any cost, the signals add nothing that holding the market would not.

The cost arithmetic made this outcome predictable. A round trip costs the same whatever the hold, while the return a trade must earn grows with its length. At 10 bps a round trip, an always-invested book loses **25.2% a year at a 1-day hold, 5.0% at 5 days, 1.26% at 20 days and 0.42% at 60 days** (cost × 252 ÷ hold). The literature matches. Anomalies trading more than about half the book a month rarely survive costs, and a buy/hold band cuts turnover by 41% on average ([Novy-Marx & Velikov](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/ToAatTC.pdf)). Turnover is the only reliable out-of-sample predictor of post-2005 net anomaly returns ([Chen & Velikov, FEDS](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2020039pap.pdf)), and the average anomaly nets **4-8 bps a month** after publication and costs ([Chen & Velikov, JFQA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/zeroing-in-on-the-expected-returns-of-anomalies/945133D5A3ECEEAF466AEE91551FD225)). Combining signals into one portfolio nets their trades and cuts the marginal cost of each by about 65% ([DeMiguel et al., via EDHEC](https://climateinstitute.edhec.edu/portfolio-perspective-multitude-firm-characteristics)). Frozen_stock does the opposite: nine strategies open and close their own positions.

The true cost level is unknown, and it decides more than the short-horizon verdicts. The published model charges 5 bps plus 5% of daily volatility plus impact, and a 1 bp fee, per side ([cost-calibration report](cost-calibration-20261106.md)). That is about 27 bps a round trip on ETFs, which the gap-fill study called pessimistic for SPY ([gap-fill report](gap-fill-earnings-drift-20261102.md)), and about 33 bps on S&P 500 names ([sector-reversal report](sector-reversal-20261107.md)). Market evidence points lower. SPY's quoted spread averaged 0.21-0.27 bps in 2024 ([SSGA](https://ssga.com/library-content/products/fund-docs/etfs/us/insights-investment-ideas/spy-capital-markets-perspective-q1-2024.pdf)), wholesalers return 44-47% of the quoted spread on S&P 500 retail orders ([Dyhrberg, Shkilko & Werner](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f187847/f187847.pdf)), and the bot's paper probes paid 0.08 bps on SPY and 1.6-1.7 bps on AAPL and MSFT per round trip ([paper audit](paper-results-audit-20261027.md)). But 85,000 simultaneous retail market orders cost **7-46 bps a round trip** depending on the broker ([Schwarz et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4189239)), and paper fills at the NBBO with no price improvement, impact or auction ([Alpaca paper trading docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/paper-trading)). The paper-fill calibration has no fitted cells locally and floors its multiplier at half the model ([cost-calibration report](cost-calibration-20261106.md)), a floor those paper spreads will probably hit, and paper fills mislead in both directions: no price improvement, but no latency or auction slippage either ([horizon note](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/horizon_cost_feasibility.md)). Cheaper fills would not rescue the failed searches. They would turn sector reversal's net positive, but a signal whose gross equals the stock's drift still loses to holding the stock. Cost does decide most of the rest of the plan. Between 5 and 25 bps a side, tranched Design A's margin over T-bills falls from 2.10 to 1.15 points a year, below the ETF note's 1.5-point bar ([ETF check](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/verify_etf.py); [output](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/verify_etf.out.txt)). At 25 bps a round trip the ranker's eight-times turnover cap costs about 2% a year ([horizon note](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/horizon_cost_feasibility.md)), half the roughly 4% a year that JKX's long-only top-500 bucket earned gross ([ML note](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/cross_sectional_ml.md)). The IBS verdict turns on whether cost sits nearer 10 or 25 bps (below). Only the passive arm and the month-end overlay, which delays buys and adds no trades, are close to cost-insensitive, so the step-1 bracket is a precondition for the step 3, 4 and 6 verdicts.

So where can a $0-budget, long-only bot plausibly have an edge at all? Not through its small size. Small orders avoid impact, but the net figures above already assume small-trader costs, the strategies institutions cannot scale are the high-turnover ones, and decay tracks publication and overfitting more than crowding ([Falck, Rej & Thesmar](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.01380)). Bank-sponsored strategies kept a median Sharpe of 0.31 live against 1.20 in backtests ([Alpha Architect on Suhonen et al.](https://alphaarchitect.com/looking-at-alternatives-avoid-complexity-and-magical-backtests/)). What survives the notes is a short list. The first item is simply not paying for turnover. The second is liquidity provision after sell-offs in the four index ETFs, which have no survivorship gap: since 2015 IBS earned **+22.0 bps** a trade [−2, +44] on SPY, QQQ, IWM and DIA ([survivorship report](survivorship-20261106.md)), and RSI(2) **+13.7 bps** [−16.9, +40.2] on the same ETFs in the scope study, which predates its market gate ([RSI(2) scope report](rsi2-scope-20261102.md)). RSI(2)'s live-scope figure of +16.6 bps [−5, +37] is not index-ETF evidence. That scope adds 29 of today's single stocks, which supply 85% of its candidates, and the point-in-time check kept it fixed, dropping only non-member days, so the figure keeps the selection survivorship the scope study itself flagged ([survivorship report](survivorship-20261106.md); [sector-reversal report](sector-reversal-20261107.md)). The research notes' own index-ETF evidence is the ETF note's SPY-only RSI(2) proxy, with a Sharpe of 0.65 at 5 bps per unit of turnover ([ETF note](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/etf_rotation.md)), which falls to 0.40 at the published model's roughly 13.5 bps a side and 0.07 at 25 bps in a rerun on the same prices ([daily blend check](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/blend_check_daily.py); [output](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/blend_check_daily.out.txt)). Over the full history the live-scope books netted only +10.4 bps (IBS) and +8.7 bps (RSI(2)) a trade, with deflated Sharpe of 0.068 and 0.051 ([sector-reversal report](sector-reversal-20261107.md)), and the horizon note rates 1-5 day IBS/RSI(2)-type reversion mostly infeasible, with no verified post-2015 net evidence ([horizon note](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/horizon_cost_feasibility.md)). The rest are slow trend as drawdown control, monthly cross-sectional ranking (documented only through 2016-2019), and predictable institutional flows at month-end. The survivors cluster in turmoil. Reversal returns rise with VIX ([Nagel](https://nber.org/papers/w17653)). Over the full history RSI(2) earned +33.5 bps a trade when VIX was high and −0.7 bps when it was low ([sector-reversal report](sector-reversal-20261107.md)). Pre-FOMC drift ran 109 bps against 9.7 bps, split by implied volatility ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/fomc-equity-drift-occurs-in-periods-of-high-uncertainty/)), and month-end rebalancing returns were 14.85% a year in high-VIX periods against 5.55% ([Harvey, Mazzoleni & Melone](https://www.edhec.edu/sites/default/files/2026-03/Scientific%20paper.%20ssrn-5122748.pdf)). A calm one-to-three-year stretch can show none of them, so every sleeve should report results by VIX bucket without being gated on VIX.

## Six directions, three lenses: agreement at the extremes, disputes in the middle

Three independent rankings scored the six directions on profit after costs, on learning speed and statistical honesty, and on implementation feasibility and risk. They agree at the extremes. Intraday ranks last or next to last on every lens, every lens treats moving slower as a frame rather than an edge, and every lens says the ML ranker and event trades should be tested offline first. They disagree in the middle. ETF trend ranks first on feasibility and second on profit but fifth on learning. The ML ranker ranks first on learning but third and fourth elsewhere. Pairs rank third on learning and last or next to last elsewhere. The table summarizes the evidence; the subsections below cite it and resolve each dispute.

### Moving slower is a frame, so adopting the frame is the decision

The profit and feasibility lenses rank the horizon shift first and second because it is the only lever whose payoff follows from arithmetic. The learning lens ranks it fourth because a frame cannot be falsified on its own, and longer holds mean fewer closed trades to learn from. Both are right about different things. The cost saving needs no test; what needs a test is any signal claiming to beat the passive arm inside the frame. So capital should move into the frame now, with idle money in a passive SPY/T-bill arm, but no trials should be spent re-running the nine existing short-horizon strategies at 40- or 60-session holds. Slowing signals whose gross already equals drift mostly buys more market exposure (−33.7 bps a trade SPY-adjusted and net of costs on the core list) while adding trials to a saturated library. The horizon note's own design supplies the rest of the frame: monthly rebalancing with buy/hold bands, a one-sided turnover budget of 4-8 times equity a year with a hard cap of 10, and a short-horizon sleeve of at most 10-20% of risk that admits a rule only when out-of-sample gross per trade is at least the larger of 30 bps and three times the measured round-trip cost **and** its net Sharpe is at least 0.5 after a 50% haircut ([horizon note](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/horizon_cost_feasibility.md)). The note meant that sleeve for event-type signals and rated IBS/RSI(2)-type 1-5 day reversion mostly infeasible. This plan departs from it only far enough to let the index-ETF IBS family face the note's full rule once, in step 4, because it is the one short-horizon family whose survivorship-free net expectancy is meaningfully positive and its gross per trade clears the 30 bps floor: about 50 bps for IBS, its +22.0 bps net plus roughly 27 bps of modeled cost. The haircut bar is stiff. A net Sharpe of 1.0 before the haircut is a date-clustered t of about 3.4 over 2015-2026, above the t ≥ 3 multiplicity hurdle the verdict research proposed ([research priorities report](Frozen%20stock%20research%20priorities.md)). IBS's interval implies a net Sharpe of about 0.55 at the published model and about 1.0 at 10 bps a round trip, and RSI(2)'s index-only interval about 0.6 even at 10 bps (my arithmetic). On today's numbers the family fails the rule at the published model; at the planning cost IBS is borderline and RSI(2) falls short.

The frame brings two costs that must be managed. A slow, mostly invested book carries equity drawdowns; SPY's maximum in 2015-2026 was −33.7% ([ETF note](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/etf_rotation.md)), so the passive arm's equity weight becomes the book's main risk dial, and it is a budget for the owner to set rather than a number the evidence fixes. The 50/50 mix matches the replay's 17.5% drawdown since 2015, but that replay ran 5% slots on today's survivors. The live rule sizes each BUY at 1% of equity and holds at most 20 positions, about 20% of equity in the market ([risk rules](../artifacts/api-server/backend/app/services/risk.py)), so even 50% SPY is a deliberate increase in risk. My rerun prices the menu the owner chooses from; each extra 10 points of SPY added about 1.1 points a year of return, and the drawdown cost is a third larger over the longer window that includes 2008-09 ([static-blend check](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/static_check.py); [output](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/static_check.out.txt)).

| 30% SPY | 5.6% | −10.7% | 4.7% | −16.5% |
| 50% SPY | 7.9% | −17.6% | 6.9% | −26.4% |

Paper accounting is the second cost. Alpaca paper does not simulate dividends ([Alpaca paper trading docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/paper-trading)), so a slow book's paper P&L understates profit by its yield. A cash leg that earns nothing in the ledger cost the trend sleeves **0.62-0.90 points a year** by itself in my rerun ([ETF check](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/verify_etf.py); [output](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/verify_etf.out.txt)). Distributions must be booked from dividend history before any slow sleeve's paper record means anything. A monthly sleeve also makes only about 12 decisions a year, so the 20-trade revert rule cannot judge it. At the bot's measured dispersion that rule reverts **48% of changes that do nothing**, and the e-process meant to replace it runs only in shadow, because it rarely decides within its 120-trade cap for effects under about 50 bps ([verdict-replay report, PR #43](verdict-replay-20261107.md)). A monthly sleeve's harm guardrail therefore has to run on its daily active P&L against the passive arm, not per trade.

### ETF trend buys crash insurance the bot should not pay for yet

The case for building ETF trend first is real. It is the only direction with a direct post-2015, net-of-cost, long-only result at this bot's cost level. It uses eight ETFs plus T-bills with clean history and no survivorship gap, and its return moves little with cost: GTAA5 went from 4.83% to 3.79% a year between zero and 25 bps per side, though Design A's thinner margin over T-bills falls from about 2.0 points to 0.84 over the same range. In 2022 the trend rules lost 3.8-6.0% while SPY lost 18.2%, and in SPY's worst tenth of months they averaged −0.92% to −1.67% against SPY's −7.08% ([ETF note](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/etf_rotation.md)). The case against is statistical and comparative. At a Sharpe near 0.4 over 11.8 years the t-statistic against cash is only 1.3-1.6, the rules lagged SPY by 8-9 points a year, and the backtest was the researcher's own unregistered run across nine rules, six rebalance offsets and three cost levels ([ETF note](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/etf_rotation.md)). The literature does not close the gap. The century of trend evidence comes from long-short futures ([Hurst, Ooi & Pedersen](https://www.trendfollowing.com/whitepaper/Century_Evidence_Trend_Following.pdf)). The SG Trend index made 1.8% a year in the 2010s ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/White-Papers/Trend-Following-Why-Now-A-Macro-Perspective.pdf?sc_lang=en)), and Virtus called the index's 12 months to April 2025 the worst in its history ([Virtus](https://mebfaber.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/the_patience_premium_5028-1747850090.pdf)). The live GMOM fund returned 3.68% a year from inception to April 2024, against 6.42% for its balanced benchmark ([SEC N-CSR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1529390/000199937124008322/cmba-ncsr_043024.htm)).

One comparison the note's own gate left out is a static SPY/T-bill blend at the same volatility. A static blend's excess Sharpe equals SPY's at any mix (exactly so with continuous rebalancing), so it marks the line for "adds value per unit of risk". I reran the note's script on its cached prices with the four weekly tranches the note prescribes, at 5, 11 and 25 bps per side. Eleven bps is about what the bot's published model charges on these ETFs (5 bps, plus 5% of a daily volatility near 1.1%, plus the 1 bp fee). I also added static arms matched to each design's volatility in each window, which means 27-28% and 39-40% SPY, rebalanced monthly; their turnover is too small for cost to matter ([ETF check](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/verify_etf.py); [output](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/verify_etf.out.txt); [static-arm calendar years](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/static_years.py); [output](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/static_years.out.txt); [original script](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/etf_rotation_backtest.py)).

In this data, trend's whole advantage over a static blend comes from one episode. Since 2015 the static arms matched both designs on drawdown, the 2022 comparison went a point or so either way, and the static arms won 2023 by 6-10 points. Over 2008-2026 the trend designs cut the maximum drawdown by 4-7 points, because the static arms' deepest fall came in the 2008-09 crash that trend sidestepped, at a cost of 0.4-1.2 points a year and a lower Sharpe. Two details weaken the case further. At 25 bps a side, Design A's 2015+ margin over T-bills falls to +1.15 points, below the note's own 1.5-point bar. Design B's median drawdown across single rebalance days is −17.3%, beyond the note's 15% limit, unless it is tranched (−13.6%). The bond legs have also changed character. SPY-IEF daily correlation ran between −0.35 and −0.51 in 2015-2020 and stands at +0.29 so far in 2026 ([ETF note](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/etf_rotation.md)). Standalone Sharpe is not the whole test, though. A sleeve with a lower Sharpe than SPY can still raise the book's Sharpe when its correlation with SPY is low enough, which is the ETF note's long-history case for trend: even at a 0.4 net Sharpe, a 20% allocation would have helped a 60/40 ([ETF note](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/etf_rotation.md)). Run that way, moving 10-30% of equity from the 50/50 arm's T-bill leg into tranched Design A, rebalanced monthly, raised the arm's Sharpe from 0.611 to 0.625-0.645 over 2008-2026 at 11 bps a side without deepening its drawdown (−25.9% to −26.3% against −26.4%). Over 2015-2026 it lowered the Sharpe (0.691-0.697 against 0.699) and deepened the drawdown by 0.8-2.2 points, and at 25 bps a side the post-2015 Sharpe fell to 0.671-0.690 ([blend check](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/blend_check.py); [output](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/blend_check.out.txt)). So the verdict depends on the window, not on whether a smaller SPY weight could stand in for trend. The resolution is that the feasibility and profit lenses are right that the machinery is the cheapest and safest build, and the learning lens is right that 12 decisions a year cannot settle the question forward. The plan therefore builds the tranched target-weight sleeve, puts the static arm in it, and judges every sleeve by one marginal rule, which an ETF sleeve must pass in both windows. Design A fails it on its post-2015 record, so it runs beside the static arm as a zero-capital shadow and is re-decided later under that rule. An owner who weights the 2008-09 evidence more heavily could still fund a small T-bill slice of A as crash insurance; that is a preference, not something the post-2015 data supports.

### A pooled ranker earns one run, after four design flaws are fixed

The learning lens ranks the cross-sectional ranker first, for a sound reason. A pooled model scored weekly across about 500 point-in-time members yields roughly 600 information-coefficient (IC) dates over 2015-2026, or about 460 in the 2018-2026 window that the coverage floor below leaves, about 110 of them non-overlapping at a 20-session label, and market beta cancels inside each one. The current long book gets far less: same-day trades correlate at 0.165, so 10 trades of one strategy on one date carry the information of about four ([liquid-universe report](liquid-universe-20261107.md)). The profit and feasibility lenses rank it third and fourth, also for a sound reason: no net-of-cost, post-2015 large-cap evidence exists. Gu, Kelly and Xiu's top-1,000 monthly R² of 0.70% is a gross forecast fit, their portfolios are long-short with turnover of 110-130% a month, and their test ends in 2016 ([Gu, Kelly & Xiu](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25398.pdf)). Jiang, Kelly and Xiu's top-500 weekly spread of 9-17 bps turns over 638-722% a month, which the ML note's cost check puts near breakeven at their own 10 bps one-way cost; their long-only top decile beat the decile average by only about 8 bps a week over 2001-2019 ([Jiang, Kelly & Xiu](https://economics.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2023-11/The%20Journal%20of%20Finance%20-%202023%20-%20JIANG%20-%20Re%25E2%2580%2590%20Imag%20in%20ing%20Price%20Trends_0.pdf)). Daily S&P 500 ML has hovered around zero after costs since 2010 ([Fischer & Krauss](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:ejores:v:270:y:2018:i:2:p:654-669)), and one ensemble went from +35.8% a year gross to −18.0% net over 2010-2015 ([Krauss, Do & Huck](https://www.iwf.rw.fau.de/files/2016/03/03-2016.pdf)). One secondary summary reports ML payoffs falling 62-80% once microcaps, unrated firms or distressed firms are excluded ([CUHK on Avramov, Cheng & Metzker](https://cbk.bschool.cuhk.edu.hk/the-limitations-of-using-ai-to-pick-stocks/)). The bot's own paused per-symbol forest made +27 bps a trade in backtest and −184 bps over 11 paper trades ([ML note](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/cross_sectional_ml.md)).

The resolution is one run, because its statistical test is the most powerful one the bot can run. A long-only sleeve with an information ratio of 0.5 over nine years has a portfolio t-statistic of only about 1.5, so no Sharpe-based gate can confirm it, while the cross-sectional IC can. But the note's design has four flaws that would make the run either uninformative or flattering, and they must be fixed before the first look. First, its deflated-Sharpe criterion at the cumulative 422-trial count cannot be passed at any plausible Sharpe; luck alone produced 0.72 at 264 trials ([strategy-discovery report](strategy-discovery-20261102.md)). The run is therefore gated on the note's IC test at the note's own bar, a Newey-West t of at least 3. That is also the two-sided 5% Bonferroni bar for about 18 trials, so it charges the four pre-declared trials for part of their overlap with rules the library already tested; the deflated Sharpe at the cumulative count is reported beside it, not used as the gate. Second, the note's 95% coverage rule would leave only 2025-2026, because point-in-time coverage was 72.0% in 2015 and 80.3% in 2018 ([survivorship report](survivorship-20261106.md)). A pre-declared 80% floor makes 2018-2026 the primary window. The names Yahoo dropped are mostly the acquired and bankrupt ones (same report), so members without history are excluded, the 2018-2026 result is labelled an upper bound, and a 2022-2026 run at 90%+ coverage is reported beside it. The 2015-2017 check, at 72-78% coverage, can veto but does not count as confirmation, and the hyperparameters, frozen on pre-2015 data at 45-71% coverage, come from a survivor-heavy sample in which a reversal or momentum model can learn that losers recover. Third, earnings history is cached only for today's names, and past members get a statistical sector map ([survivorship report](survivorship-20261106.md); [sector-reversal report](sector-reversal-20261107.md)). Earnings-date features would therefore let the model learn survivorship, so they should be dropped and one sector method used for every name. Fourth, the note's turnover cap of 40% weekly book replacement is about 21 times equity a year, two to five times the frame's budget; a cap of 8 times a year means about 15% replacement a week. Two of these changes loosen the note's rule, the 80% coverage floor and the swap of the cumulative-count deflated Sharpe for a t ≥ 3 gate (the four trials are still logged into the library), and they are labelled as such; the rest tighten it.

The base rate is still poor. The likeliest result is a smoothed blend of momentum and low volatility with a net information ratio near 0.3-0.5 ([ML note](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/cross_sectional_ml.md)), and cost-blind forecasts tend to lean on fleeting characteristics that do not survive costs ([Jensen, Kelly, Malamud & Pedersen](https://sfi.ch/en/publications/machine-learning-and-the-implementable-efficient-frontier)). The run belongs on a laptop, not the production worker. scikit-learn's histogram gradient boosting is already a pinned dependency and LightGBM is not ([requirements.txt](../artifacts/api-server/backend/requirements.txt)). A negative result would close only the price-and-volume branch. The horizon note puts the cost-surviving evidence in low-turnover value, profitability and quality tilts, feasible on costs but slow to verify, and names multi-signal tilts as the main gap for the bot's own tests ([horizon note](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/horizon_cost_feasibility.md)); the ML note lists free SEC filings as a second phase ([ML note](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/cross_sectional_ml.md)). That family is deferred, not closed, until the SEC data's filing timestamps are confirmed usable point in time, which the ML note left unverified.

### Events shrink to an overlay; pairs and intraday get a kill test or nothing

The learning lens ranks events second because per-stock events repeat fast: about 2,000 S&P 500 earnings reports and 1,380 ex-dividend dates a year ([event note](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/event_calendar_trades.md); [QuanterLab](https://quanterlab.com/research/dividend-capture-loses-the-week-before-the-ex-date-pays)). The other lenses rank them third and fourth because the fast items rest on weak priors and the strong item learns slowest. Both observations hold, and together they shrink the direction to an overlay. The best-evidenced item is month-end rebalancing pressure. A one-standard-deviation calendar signal predicts about −17 bps for equities and +2 bps for bonds the next day, about −19 bps stocks minus bonds, and the long-short futures version kept a net Sharpe near 1 over 1997-2023. But its skew is 5.23, the 2008-09 crisis and March 2020 are major contributors, and no post-2015 split is reported ([Harvey, Mazzoleni & Melone](https://www.edhec.edu/sites/default/files/2026-03/Scientific%20paper.%20ssrn-5122748.pdf)). There have been about 130 month-ends since 2015. At the note's assumed 1.2% daily spread volatility, the full published spread effect would show a t-statistic of about 1.8 over that span, and about 0.9 after a 50% decay haircut (my arithmetic). It can therefore be accepted only on a 2002-2026 replication with a 2015+ holdout. A long-only book can use it only to delay equity buys, which is worth roughly 17 bps per standard deviation of signal on the slice of equity bought that day. The fast items are weak. The US earnings premium has not differed from zero since 2004, a finding seen only in the abstract ([Heitz et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3296537)). The week before an ex-dividend date beat SPY by 10 bps per event, with an interval of −2 to +23 bps, in one practitioner study where 13 of 18 windows favored the unselected arm ([QuanterLab](https://quanterlab.com/research/dividend-capture-loses-the-week-before-the-ex-date-pays)). S&P additions earned an insignificant 0.8% in 2010-2020 ([Greenwood & Sammon](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w30748/w30748.pdf)), and the FOMC even-week cycle failed out of sample in 2017-2023 ([Uppal](https://aliuppal.me/files/papers/Ali_Uppal_CB_Cycles.pdf)). In a book that holds 20-60 sessions, tilts of 10-15 bps are noise. They get zero-cost logs, sized by the event note's own arithmetic. At the plan's t ≥ 2.5 bar the pre-ex-dividend effect needs about 3,900 events and the earnings premium about 6,900, roughly three and three and a half years of raw events, and earnings-season clustering lowers the effective count further, a gap the note flags itself (my arithmetic from the event note). So the ex-dividend log is backfilled over 2015-2026 for point-in-time payers, as the note recommends, and the earnings log, a market-adjusted pre-report return for every S&P 500 report, gets a verdict date set by its effective event count rather than by the calendar, since its point-in-time history is unverified.

The learning lens ranks pairs third because beta-hedged residual trades correlate less: on the bot's own trades, same-day correlation falls from 0.165 raw to 0.080 after removing SPY ([liquid-universe report](liquid-universe-20261107.md)). The other two lenses drop pairs, and the bot's data supports them. The pairs note describes its recommended lane as sector-relative reversal with the beta hedged out. Its deciding question is whether long-only reversal failed because of beta noise or because no residual edge exists ([pairs note](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/pairs_stat_arb.md)). The sector-reversal replay answers it for the version it tested: long entries on a relative dip, every variant filtered to stocks above their 200-day average, with a statistically assigned sector ETF for 661 of the 691 stocks ([sector-reversal report](sector-reversal-20261107.md)). There gross equaled the stock's own drift in every window, so a hedge starts near zero. It then pays four fills per pair round trip, roughly 56-60 bps under the published model (33 bps on the stock plus 23-27 bps on the ETF), against the note's assumed 4-12 bps. External evidence is thin. Daily S&P 500 stat-arb lost 14-25% a year after 5 bps half-turn costs in 2010-2015 ([Krauss, Do & Huck](https://www.iwf.rw.fau.de/files/2016/03/03-2016.pdf)). Classic distance-method pairs in the most liquid quartile earned only 0.188% a month over 2004-2023, with no cost deduction ([Zhu](https://economics.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2024-05/Zhu_Pairs_Trading.pdf)). The positive 36-41 bps a month for clustered S&P 500 pairs comes from a secondary review ([Sun on Rotondi & Russo](https://www.wne.uw.edu.pl/application/files/8917/5759/3293/WNE_WP484.pdf)). Paper omits borrow fees and the dividends a short owes ([Alpaca paper trading docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/paper-trading)), and Rule 201 restricts shorting on crash days ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/news/press/2010/2010-26.htm)). The short pilot is capped at four symbols, $1,000 an order and $3,000 gross ([config](../artifacts/api-server/backend/app/core/config.py)). And because the edge arrives in crisis bursts, confirming a 0.3-0.4% per-trade edge takes 1,000-1,700 trades ([pairs note](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/pairs_stat_arb.md)). The resolution is no build. One kill test, which hedges the existing 18,469 sector-reversal trades with their sector ETF, costs hours and replaces inference with measurement, provided it is read by VIX bucket and crisis sub-period, because the uptrend filter removes much of the sell-off regime where the note expects the residual edge to live ([pairs note](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/pairs_stat_arb.md)). A fail closes the hedged uptrend version; the lane closes for good only if one variant without the filter also fails.

All three lenses drop intraday, for reasons that are independent of each other. The overnight drift earned about 3.7% a year before 2021 and close to zero since ([NY Fed](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/07/the-disappearing-overnight-drift/)). A naive SPY close-to-open trade lost 32% after costs over 1993-2020, against +717% before costs ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/trading-costs-wipe-out-the-overnight-return-anomaly/), from a search snippet). The best-known net-of-cost SPY intraday momentum strategy fell from a Sharpe of 1.33 to 0.39 out of sample in 2024-2026 ([Paz](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7290621), from a search snippet), and a blog study of 1,085 SPX sessions found the first-to-last half-hour effect flat in every year of 2022-2026 ([gex.live](https://dev.to/firmtape/intraday-momentum-is-dead-in-the-0dte-era-we-measured-it-on-1085-spx-sessions-43g0)). The free data plan gives IEX-only real-time data with a 30-symbol websocket cap and SIP data delayed 15 minutes, while full real-time data costs $99 a month ([Alpaca market data docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/about-market-data-api)). That rules out the opening-range "stocks in play" selection, which screens more than 7,000 names in real time ([Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz](https://ideas.repec.org/p/chf/rpseri/rp2498.html)). Paper does not run the auctions; on one day 59.7% of paper market-on-open orders were cancelled ([Alpaca forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/recently-seeing-partial-fills-on-market-opg-order-s-p500-stocks/13906)). Daily round trips also carry about 25% a year of cost drag at 10 bps. The one live idea is to buy the close after a large down day and sell the next open. That is the overnight bounce after sell-offs ([Boyarchenko, Larsen & Whelan](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/staff_reports/sr917)) that IBS and RSI(2) already harvest, so it becomes a single variant trial in that family. It must be decided on prices from before Alpaca's 3:50 pm closing-auction cutoff ([Alpaca orders docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/orders-at-alpaca)) and split before and after 2021.

## Decision: a slow book with a passive core, one gated sleeve, two drops

Direction 1 is chosen as the frame and the live change, and direction 3's tranched target-weight machinery is built as its first component. The capital in that machinery goes to a passive SPY/T-bill arm, held in IVV or VOO so that no sleeve trades the same symbol, at an equity weight the owner sets as a risk budget. Until the owner sets it, the arm starts at 20%, about the most equity the live book can hold today. Fifty percent would be a deliberate increase in risk, with static-blend drawdowns of 17.6% since 2015 and 26.4% since 2008, and each extra 10 points adds about 1.1 points of return and 3.4 points of drawdown since 2015. Design A runs beside it as a zero-capital challenger. The IBS family on the four index ETFs, with RSI(2) cut back to the same ETFs, is the only short-horizon candidate. It keeps its current paper size until step 4 and takes 10-20% of risk only if it passes the horizon note's full admission rule there; on today's numbers it fails at the published cost model and is borderline at 10 bps a round trip, so shadow is the likelier outcome. Its idle capital sits in the passive arm. Direction 6 gets one pre-registered offline run as the last remaining test of price-based equity alpha in this space; a low-turnover fundamental tilt is deferred, not closed. Direction 4 gets one replication (month-end flow, as a timing overlay) and zero-cost logs. Direction 2 is dropped entirely, and direction 5 is dropped as a profit lane after one kill test; the short pilot remains a plumbing and cost-measurement lane.

Other work stops too. New 1-20 session long-only single-stock search families end. The seven broad technical strategies (MA crossover, MACD, ensemble, RSI(14), Bollinger, channel breakout, trend pullback) lose capital and move to shadow. Six of the seven have negative point-in-time expectancy since 2015 and none has an interval above zero ([survivorship report](survivorship-20261106.md)). RSI(2)'s 29-stock leg joins them, since its positive record is on today's list, and returns only if one run on the 707-name point-in-time universe passes step 4's rule. GEM, top-k rotation and levered risk parity are dropped ([ETF note](../research_notes/Retail%20bot%20edge%20feasibility/etf_rotation.md)), as are daily and weekly ML variants and deep networks. Index-addition, option-expiry, pre-holiday, Treasury-auction, FOMC even-week, leveraged-ETF-flow and ex-day dividend trades are dropped as well.

Adoptions are therefore provisional by construction. They rest on point-in-time replay under the rules above, and forward paper does two jobs only. It checks implementation: tracking against the replay, realized costs and booked distributions. It also runs harm guardrails. The always-valid betting e-process at a 10-20% harm level that the earlier verdict-engine research recommended ([research priorities report](Frozen%20stock%20research%20priorities.md)) now runs in shadow, because at the bot's measured inputs it crosses within 50-60 trades only for effects near ±150 bps, so fixed_20 stays the acting judge, the short-horizon sleeve included ([verdict-replay report, PR #43](verdict-replay-20261107.md)). A monthly sleeve would need four to five years to reach 50-60 decisions, which is why its guardrail runs on daily active P&L against the passive arm instead. Confirmations across families come only from forward e-values under e-LOND, whose false-discovery guarantee holds under arbitrary dependence between tests ([Xu & Ramdas](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v238/xu24a.html)), and at the measured inputs they will be rare. Steps 3, 4 and 6 are not fresh experiments in the strict sense: the ETF and IBS/RSI(2) numbers have already been looked at, RSI(2)'s scope was chosen on the same post-2015 replays that step 4 re-runs, and the ranker's features overlap the reversal and momentum rules the discovery library already tested. The pre-registration binds what happens next, not what is already known.

## Conclusion

The seven failures were less seven separate misses than one missing yardstick. The book-level searches asked whether a change beat the existing book, and the rule searches asked whether a candidate beat zero or the stock's own drift. None asked whether the book beat the passive exposure it could have held at the same drawdown. Judged that way, the replayed book's 4% a year trails the 7.9% a static blend earned at the same drawdown, though only at the published cost model and before survivor flattery is removed; how large that loss is stays uncertain, but at any cost the turnover bought no timing value. The cheapest improvement available to this bot is a benchmark, not a signal. Once that benchmark exists, "self-improving" stops meaning a weekly hunt for rules that beat nothing and starts meaning risk allocation among sleeves that must beat the bot's own default. Idle cash and turnover then show up as losses the engine can see, and no trial is needed to remove them.

The remaining uncertainty is concentrated in one input. Real execution cost decides the short-horizon sleeve, the trend sleeve's margin over T-bills, which roughly halves between 5 and 25 bps a side, and the ranker, whose turnover cap costs about half its expected edge at 25 bps; only the passive arm and the month-end overlay are close to cost-insensitive. The bot can therefore build the engine and the passive arm now, but the step-1 cost bracket, and measured cost where it exists, has to come before the step 3, 4 and 6 verdicts. The fastest honest learning now comes from cross-sectional replay rather than forward paper, since one ranker run can say more than years of paper trading a monthly sleeve. The realistic ceiling is passive-like return at a chosen drawdown, plus perhaps a point or two a year if a sleeve passes. The most likely outcome of the ranker test is a documented negative, and even that has value, because it closes the last open branch of price-based equity alpha for a long-only, large-cap, $0-data bot, leaving low-turnover fundamental tilts as the one family still untested.
