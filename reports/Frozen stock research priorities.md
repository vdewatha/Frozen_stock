# Fix the verdict engine before hunting edges

The research that would help Frozen_stock most is not a new signal. It is a better way of deciding whether a change worked. At a realistic per-trade standard deviation of about 2%, the current rule ("revert if expectancy is at least 10 bps worse after 20 forward trades") reverts about **41% of changes that do nothing** and about **9% of changes that are truly 50 bps better**. Effects under about 50 bps cannot be judged at 20 trades by any method. Always-valid e-processes on paired shadow differences can bound false reverts at 10% and false adoptions through e-value online FDR, and they still cut large losers in roughly 20-90 trades. The second lever is evidence rate. Throughput is limited by slots, not symbols, and widening the exploration lane to the 200-300 most liquid point-in-time S&P 500 names with more exploration slots cuts time-to-30-effective-trades per strategy from about 37 weeks to roughly 4-11. Among published anomalies, only **sector- or industry-relative short-term reversal** has credible post-2015 support in large caps at this horizon. Pre-FOMC drift survives only when implied volatility is high, and turn-of-month survives only as a small timing tilt. PEAD in large caps has been dead since about 2006. Free causal data beyond price and news is thin. Overnight/intraday decomposition (free, already in the bot's bars) and FINRA daily short volume (free, backfillable to 2018) are the only near-term additions with real evidence. Options skew and analyst revisions have strong evidence but no free history, so collectors should start now. On execution, Alpaca paper does not simulate the opening auction at all, so any OPG experiment must be scored against the official SIP open rather than paper fills.

## The 20-trade verdict is a coin flip, and e-processes can replace it

### Twenty trades cannot see edges below about 50 bps

The arithmetic is unforgiving. With a 2% per-trade standard deviation, the standard error of a 20-trade mean is 200/√20 ≈ **44.7 bps**. A change with zero true effect therefore lands at least 10 bps below the old expectancy with probability Φ(−10/44.7) ≈ **0.41**. A truly +50 bps change does so with probability Φ(−60/44.7) ≈ **0.09** (in-house normal-approximation arithmetic; the extra "loss cause no rarer" condition lowers both rates by an unmeasured amount). The fixed-sample formula gives the scale of the problem: at 2% standard deviation, one-sided α = 0.05 and 80% power need about **99 trades for a 50 bps edge, 619 for 20 bps and 2,474 for 10 bps**. No sequential method changes this. Sequential methods save trades only when effects are large, and they stop wasting trades on clear cases. The forward stage should therefore be rebuilt as a fast harm guardrail plus a slow, honest confirmation, not as a 20-trade judge.

### Always-valid inference lets the bot peek every day without inflating errors

A confidence sequence is a sequence of intervals that covers the true mean simultaneously at every time, so it stays valid at any data-dependent stopping point ([confseq on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/confseq/)). The mixture sequential probability ratio test (mSPRT) of Johari, Pekelis and Walsh turns this into always-valid p-values. Its mixing scale should be "in the ballpark" of the expected effect: being off by one order of magnitude costs under 5% power, and being off by two costs about 20% power and 40% run length ([Johari et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/1512.04922v3)). Eppo's production version is an asymptotic confidence sequence with plug-in variance and a tuning sample size, which Eppo sets at 10,000. That is far too large for a bot that sees tens of trades ([Eppo](https://docs.geteppo.com/statistics/confidence-intervals/statistical-nitty-gritty/)). The theory behind plug-in versions is asymptotic only, so coverage at 20-60 fat-tailed trades is not guaranteed ([Waudby-Smith et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.06476)).

The assumption-light option is a betting construction. Waudby-Smith and Ramdas build confidence sequences for bounded data that need only a constant conditional mean, not independence, and they report that these "empirically vastly outperform" Hoeffding and empirical-Bernstein bounds ([Waudby-Smith and Ramdas](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.09686)). For heavy tails, Catoni-style sequences need only a known variance bound ([Wang and Ramdas](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.01250)). One derivation matters especially for Frozen_stock. For the null "mean per-trade return ≤ 0", the wealth process K_t = ∏(1 + λ_i X_i) with predictable bets λ_i < 1/B is a test supermartingale whenever every return X_i ≥ −B. Long-only, unlevered trades satisfy X ≥ −1 with certainty, and a practical B (the worst plausible single-trade loss, say 10-15%) allows near-optimal bets. A mixture over a fixed λ grid is still valid and needs no tuning. Bayesian monitoring is not a shortcut. Bayesian tests stay valid under continuous monitoring only with "proper" stopping rules ([Deng, Lu and Chen](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05549)), and a fixed posterior threshold under peek-and-stop does not control false positives ([Molas 2025](https://alexmolas.com/2025/10/30/bayesian-ab-test-peeking.html)).

The research team's own Monte Carlo (Student-t(3) returns, 2% standard deviation, 4,000 replications) gives a feel for stopping times. Under a true zero effect, the mSPRT falsely stopped in only 1.5-2.1% of runs by 600 trades. For a true +50 bps edge, its median stop was about **85 trades**. For +100 bps the median was about **22 trades**. For −50 bps harm the median was about **88 trades**. These are in-house simulations under stationary returns and a perfect shadow pairing, not literature results.

### Group-sequential designs win only when a hard maximum is enforced

Spotify's comparison found group-sequential tests with alpha-spending reach about **0.90 power, against 0.67-0.77 for mSPRT**, when the maximum sample size is known. Overrunning that maximum pushed false positives to about 0.07-0.08 against a 0.05 target ([Spotify Engineering](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2023/03/choosing-sequential-testing-framework-comparisons-and-discussions)). Frozen_stock's "confirmed at 20, watch to 60" habit is exactly that kind of unplanned extra look. In the team's simulation, a 110-trade O'Brien-Fleming design cut expected trades by about 29% at +50 bps and 57% at +100 bps, and saved nothing at zero effect. A group-sequential design suits the bot only if the maximum is a hard stop. Otherwise always-valid methods are the safer default, because they tolerate indefinite monitoring.

### Only e-value online FDR survives the bot's data reuse

The engine generates an open-ended stream of hypotheses scored on nearly the same history each week, among strategies that share market exposure. Classic online FDR rules such as LORD, SAFFRON and ADDIS assume independence or limited dependence, and the onlineFDR package that implements them is GPL-3 ([onlineFDR manual](https://free.nchc.org.tw/bioconductor/packages/devel/bioc/manuals/onlineFDR/man/onlineFDR.pdf)). e-LOND controls online FDR under **arbitrary, unknown dependence**, and the motivating case is many hypotheses evaluated on one dataset ([Xu and Ramdas, AISTATS 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v238/xu24a.html)). Online e-BH uniformly improves on it ([Fischer, Xu and Ramdas](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.20683v3)). Asynchronous online testing formalises streams in which each test is itself sequential and may never finish ([Zrnic, Ramdas and Jordan, JMLR 2021](https://JMLR.org/papers/v22/19-910.html)). The workable design treats the backtest as a screen and counts a "discovery" only when the forward e-process for an adopted change crosses its online level. Forward data arrives after selection, so it stays valid however much the backtest was mined.

The cost is real. With an FDR budget of 0.10 and a first-test weight near 0.07, the first confirmation needs an e-value of about 140, or roughly 160 trades at the optimal growth rate for a 50 bps edge (in-house arithmetic; the e-LOND formulas must be checked against the paper before coding). Most small-effect adoptions will therefore remain "provisional" indefinitely. That is the honest outcome; the alternative is silently accumulating false adoptions. Reverts restore the status quo, so they need no FDR budget, only a per-experiment harm level of 0.10-0.20.

The backtest stage also needs a multiplicity hurdle. Harvey, Liu and Zhu argue a new factor needs **t > 3.0, not 2.0** ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2513152)). The deflated Sharpe ratio corrects for the expected maximum Sharpe among N zero-skill trials and for non-normal returns ([Bailey and López de Prado](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)). The current "every window not lower" rule has no such correction, and Frozen_stock runs 342-trial libraries. The hurdle should use the cumulative trial count per strategy, not this week's count.

| True effect | Current rule (20 trades) | Proposed e-process rule |
|---|---|---|
| −100 bps harm | caught at 20 trades | reverts at about 20-30 trades, error bounded |
| −50 bps harm | noisy verdict at 20 | median about 88 trades, valid |
| No effect | reverts about 41% of the time | at most 10% false reverts; non-inferiority call at cap |
| +50 bps | about 9% false reverts | confirms at median about 85 trades (α 0.05), slower under e-LOND |
| +5 to +20 bps | coin flip | stays provisional; needs 600-10,000 trades |

### Experiments for the verdict engine

The first experiment is a measurement the bot can run today from its own logs. Compute the realised per-trade standard deviation, the worst single-trade loss and gain, and the variance of **paired shadow differences**, then compare that variance with raw per-trade variance. For a gate change, the paired difference is the return of trades the new gate admits minus the shadow return of trades it blocks, net of costs and minus β·SPY over the same holding window. Common market exposure cancels, and every blocked trade becomes free evidence. These numbers set B, the λ grid and the trade cap. Everything in the table above scales with them.

The second experiment is an offline replay of every past adopt and revert decision. Run three judges side by side on the same forward trades: the 20-trade rule, a two-sided betting e-process (harm on −x at α = 0.10, improvement on x at the e-LOND level), and a 110-trade O'Brien-Fleming design. Count how many past verdicts flip. Bets must use only trades that closed before the current trade opened, and same-day entries should be batched into one observation to avoid partial-path leakage. Once that replay looks sane, the bot can switch to the e-process judge in shadow mode, with a 120-trade cap after which a change is kept only if the confidence-sequence lower bound exceeds −10 bps.

The third experiment is a retroactive deflation of every adopted change, using cumulative trial counts with a t ≥ 3 or deflated-Sharpe-above-0.95 hurdle on the paired difference, to see which current adoptions would not have passed. The fourth fits a hierarchical (empirical-Bayes) model to realised versus backtest-expected gains across all adopted changes. Its slope, with an uncertainty band, replaces the audit's running calibration ratio. A skeptical prior of about ±20 bps shrinks a 20-trade forward mean only about 17% of the way toward the observed value (in-house conjugate arithmetic). That is a further reason never to read 20-trade results as verdicts.

## Only sector-relative reversal and conditional calendar slivers survive in large caps

### Post-publication decay is large and harshest in value-weighted large caps

Chen and Velikov find gross anomaly returns decay about **50% after publication, and 72% when restricted to post-2005 data** ([Chen and Velikov, Fed FEDS 2020-039](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2020039pap.pdf)). McLean and Pontiff report returns **26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication** across 97 predictors ([McLean and Pontiff](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)). Net of effective spreads, post-publication decay and the modern trading era, the average anomaly earns "a measly 4 bps per month", the best about 10 bps, and combinations about 20 bps ([Chen and Velikov, JFQA 2023](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/zeroing-in-on-the-expected-returns-of-anomalies/945133D5A3ECEEAF466AEE91551FD225)). Novy-Marx and Velikov's rule of thumb is that costs eat about 1% of monthly one-sided turnover, so anomalies trading more than 50% a month rarely survive ([NBER w20721](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20721.pdf); [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/2017/05/trading-costs-destroy-factor-investing/)). Under NYSE breakpoints and value weights, the setting closest to liquid large caps, about **65% of anomalies fail a t ≥ 1.96 test** ([Chen and Zimmermann, CFR](https://cfr.ivo-welch.info/published/papers/chen2021open.pdf)).

Jensen, Kelly and Pedersen push the other way. Their Bayesian replication finds most factor themes hold, with rates above 75% in 10 of 13 themes; the exceptions are **Seasonality, Leverage and Size** ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/is-there-a-replication-crisis-in-finance/)). They also report replication across the size distribution, although their capped value-weighting de-emphasises the largest stocks ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/re-examining-equity-factor-research-replicability/)). The two views reconcile. Value, momentum, quality and low-risk premia exist, but they are monthly-to-annual long-short factors. A long-only, 1-20 session bot can use them only as tilts when ranking candidates, not as standalone trades.

### Short-term reversal survives when measured against its industry

Generic short-term reversal (buying last month's raw losers) is fading. Blitz, van der Grient and Honarvar find that **industry-adjusted and residual reversal more than doubled generic reversal's gross Sharpe ratio, "with no decay"** through 2022. Their sample is about 1,745 MSCI World stocks a year, but the results are gross of costs at roughly **2,400% annual turnover** ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/reviving-short-term-reversal/)). Hameed and Mian find intra-industry monthly reversals persist and remain significant even among large-cap, liquid, institutionally held stocks, and are stronger after market declines ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v50y2015i1-2p89-117_00.html)). de Groot, Huij and Zhou find reversal earns **30-50 bps per week net of costs**, with the cost drag coming mainly from small caps ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jbfina:v:36:y:2012:i:2:p:371-382)). Nagel shows reversal returns are liquidity-provision returns whose expected size is predictable by VIX and rises "enormously" in turmoil ([NBER w17653](https://nber.org/papers/w17653)).

The steelman against this view is Cheng, Hameed, Subrahmanyam and Titman: outside micro-caps, the standard reversal strategy earns insignificant returns in the recent period ([ABFER 2014](https://www.abfer.org/media/abfer-events-2014/annual-conference/investment-finance/short-term-reversals-and-the-efficiency-of-liquidity-provision.pdf)). The difference is construction. Raw reversal is mostly dead in large caps, while industry-relative and residual reversal is not. That matters for Frozen_stock's RSI(2), IBS and pullback rules, which today rank on raw weakness. Ranking on sector-relative weakness targets the idiosyncratic overshoot that the literature says still pays. It also lowers cross-trade correlation, which feeds directly into evidence rate.

There is indirect support for fading short moves in index instruments too. Kurth, Eisler, Rej and Bouchaud find that "short-term trends have ceased to deliver reliable returns" since about 2009, collapsing on small-tick contracts ([arXiv 2607.01550](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01550)). The paper does not test mean reversion or name equity ETFs, so it supports ETF mean reversion only by inference. It does lower the prior on short-horizon breakout rules.

### Calendar effects survive only conditionally

The classic [0,+3] turn-of-month window shows no significant effect on US equity ETFs in recent data. The wider [−3,+3] window still shows about **5-12 bps a day** above other days, though it is declining. A long-only turn-of-month ETF strategy lowers CAGR but improves drawdowns and skew ([QuantSeeker](https://www.quantseeker.com/p/turn-of-the-month-strategies-do-they)). The pre-FOMC drift "essentially disappeared after 2015" ([Kurov, Wolfe and Gilbert](https://www.skidmore.edu/economics/documents/KurovWolfeGilbert-TheDisappearingPre-FOMC-Announce-Drift-200914.pdf)). It is concentrated in high-uncertainty periods: about **109 bps when implied volatility is above its prior median, against 9.7 bps below** ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/fomc-equity-drift-occurs-in-periods-of-high-uncertainty/)). Frozen_stock's Fed-calendar BUY blackout may be forgoing exactly that sliver.

### Effects to stop spending trials on

For large stocks, PEAD has been absent since 2006 ([Martineau, CFR](https://cfr.ivo-welch.info/published/papers/martineau2021rest.pdf)). A 2025 working paper finds the drift factor's t-statistic falls from 2.18 to **1.43 once microcaps are excluded** ([UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/is-post-earnings-announcement-drift-a-thing-again)). That matches the bot's own "PEAD blocked by survivorship" result. Heston-Sadka-style same-month seasonality has only pre-2011 large-cap evidence ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/Screener/Details/125)), and Jensen, Kelly and Pedersen flag seasonality as a weak theme. Overnight/intraday decomposition is an open lens: momentum profits accrue overnight and other strategies' profits intraday ([Lou, Polk and Skouras](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/TugOfWar.pdf)), but no post-2015 large-cap test was found. Pre-holiday, earnings-announcement premium, 52-week-high and MAX effects went untested in this research.

### Experiments on surviving anomalies

All of these can run on existing data. The highest-value test is a **sector-relative reversal candidate** on the point-in-time S&P 500 replay: buy names whose 1-5 day return is lowest relative to their SPDR sector ETF, optionally only in an uptrend, with a buy/hold hysteresis exit (for example enter at sector-relative RSI(2) < 5 and exit above 70 rather than 50). Novy-Marx and Velikov identify such bands as the most effective cost mitigation. The same replay should compare raw versus sector-relative ranking inside the existing RSI(2), IBS and pullback rules. Run it separately on calm and high-VIX days, since Nagel predicts the edge concentrates in turmoil. That interacts with the bot's existing VIX and SPY-trend gates.

The second test is a conditional pre-FOMC long: enter before scheduled FOMC announcements only when VIX is above its trailing median, against the current blackout. It is a single pre-registered trial with a direct source prediction. The third is a [−3,+3] turn-of-month overlay that changes entry timing or size for existing mean-reversion signals rather than trading on its own. A useful offline oracle sits alongside these. Open Source Asset Pricing publishes free replicated portfolio returns, including value-weighted and NYSE-breakpoint variants ([openassetpricing.com](https://www.openassetpricing.com/)). Checking a theme's post-2015 large-cap returns there before spending engine trials on it reduces the deflation penalty those trials would incur.

## Free causal data beyond prices is thin; overnight splits and short volume lead

### Overnight and intraday components are free, point-in-time and already in the bars

Lou, Polk and Skouras find that all abnormal momentum returns occur overnight, while other strategies' premia accrue intraday, consistent with institutions trading intraday against momentum ([LSE Research Online](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/87481)). Akbas, Boehmer, Jiang and Koch find that frequent "positive overnight, negative daytime" reversals predict lower returns. Stocks with abnormally frequent negative daytime reversals outperform for up to six months without later reversal. Their sample covers US stocks from 1993 to 2017 and holds in NYSE 1926-1962 data as well ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:145:y:2022:i:3:p:850-875)). Replication outside the US is mixed ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/pacfin/v74y2022ics0927538x22001044.html)). These are monthly cross-sectional effects on broad samples, so for 45 megacaps they belong in the meta-label classifier rather than as standalone rules. They cost hours to build from existing OHLC. Dividend ex-dates at the open need care.

### FINRA short volume is free and backfillable, with signal in the extremes

FINRA posts daily short-sale volume files by 6:00 p.m. ET on the trade date, occasionally with a later "Updated" file ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/finra-data/browse-catalog/short-sale-volume-data/daily-short-sale-volume-files)). The consolidated file starts in August 2018, and per-venue files go back further. The public series still predicted **negative returns over 2010-2015**, when it was published in real time ([Lehigh-hosted JFE paper](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/Short_Final_Version.pdf)). The predictive content sits in extremes, especially unusually **low** short volume ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/short-positions/)), which is the useful tail for a long-only bot. The data covers off-exchange prints only and is dominated by market-maker hedging ([ORTEX](https://public.ortex.com/why-daily-short-volumes-are-often-misinterpreted/)), so a per-symbol z-score against its own 60-day history is required. Twice-monthly short interest is published about 7 business days after settlement ([FINRA SR-FINRA-2026-012](https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2026-05/SR-FINRA-2026-012.pdf)). Keying it on settlement date would build in a 1.5-week lookahead. The proprietary benchmark is strong: heavily shorted NYSE stocks underperform by **1.16% over 20 trading days** ([Boehmer, Jones and Zhang](https://www.johnhcochrane.com/s/boehmer_jones_zhang_shorts.pdf)). In mega-caps, though, short interest is a small share of float, so the expected large-cap value is low to moderate.

### Insider filings have the best historical evidence and the weakest large-cap case

EDGAR is free and point-in-time if each filing is keyed on its acceptance timestamp. Forms 3/4/5 accepted up to 10:00 p.m. ET are disseminated the same day, and access is capped at 10 requests per second with a declared User-Agent ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-search-assistance/accessing-edgar-data)). Cohen, Malloy and Pomorski show that "opportunistic" insider trades earn **82 bps a month value-weighted**, while routine trades earn about zero, but their sample ends in 2007 ([NBER Digest](https://www.nber.org/digest/apr11/w16454.html)). The insider buy-versus-sell gap is "much weaker for large companies" ([IBKR Campus](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/traders-insight/securities/stocks/what-corporate-insider-buying-can-tell-investors-evidence-from-academic-research/)). No post-2010 large-cap replication was found. In a 45-name book, open-market insider buys are rare, so the realistic use is a sparse meta-label feature tested on the wider point-in-time universe. 8-K item flags come through the same pipeline. Event-day reactions dominate, with some item-level drift ([Israelsen 2018](https://www.lehigh.edu/~jms408/Israelsen_2018.pdf); [Lerman and Livnat](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/reaccs/v15y2010i4d10.1007_s11142-009-9114-7.html)). An "officer departure in the last 5 days, skip entry" flag is cheap to add once the pipeline exists. The MIT-licensed `edgartools` parses both form types ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/edgartools/5.31.0/)).

### Options and analyst revisions have strong evidence but no free history

The strongest short-horizon academic evidence in this whole area has no free backfill. Stocks with relatively expensive calls beat those with expensive puts by **50 bps a week** ([Cremers and Weinbaum](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/deviations-from-putcall-parity-and-stock-return-predictability/D9BA8F97580328AAFD7988B092FE5D50)). A steep volatility smirk predicts low returns for at least six months ([Xing, Zhang and Zhao](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/what-does-the-individual-option-volatility-smirk-tell-us-about-future-equity-returns/ECFD16BA9ACBDC8D577D1BD866FBEA72)). Cremers and Weinbaum note the mispricing declined over their sample, and these broad-sample effects are plausibly smaller in mega-caps. CBOE's free aggregate put/call files run only from 2006 to 2019 ([CBOE](https://ww2.cboe.com/us/options/market_statistics/historical_data)), and no free single-stock implied-volatility history was found. yfinance's `eps_trend` and `eps_revisions` are rolling windows relative to today and cannot be backfilled ([yfinance docs](https://ranaroussi.github.io/yfinance/reference/api/yfinance.Ticker.html)). The only free route for both is to snapshot daily from now on and wait 12-24 months. 13F holdings arrive up to 45 days stale. WallStreetBets evidence is meme-stock-centric ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/investing-expertise/wallstreetbets-contributors-and-users-skilled)). Neither suits this bot.

### Experiments on new data

The immediate experiment adds overnight return, intraday return, a 20-day count of negative daytime reversals, and a 20-day overnight-minus-intraday spread as meta-label features and rule-language primitives. The PIT S&P 500 replay then tests whether they improve out-of-sample filtering of RSI(2) and pullback entries. The second experiment needs one download but no waiting. Backfill FINRA consolidated short volume from 2018, build a per-symbol short-volume-ratio z-score keyed on the first-seen file, and test "very low short-volume ratio" as an entry tilt and "very high" as an avoid filter. The third is a one-off engineering job: backfill Form 4s for point-in-time S&P 500 names, classify routine versus opportunistic insiders from 3+ years of history, and test cluster buys as a sparse feature. The fourth costs a day of work now and pays later: start daily collectors for option chains (45 names, a few expiries) and `eps_trend`/`eps_revisions`, storing raw snapshots with capture timestamps. Check Yahoo's terms first. Every published magnitude in this section should be haircut by about 58% for post-publication decay before it is compared with costs.

## Paper trading cannot test the opening auction, so measure against the SIP open

### OPG orders reach the real auction live, but paper only imitates them

Alpaca's `opg` time-in-force covers market-on-open and limit-on-open orders. These execute only in the opening auction and must be submitted before 9:28 ET or after 7:00 p.m. ET the evening before. On-open orders route to the primary exchange, and bracket orders accept only `day` or `gtc`, so an OPG entry cannot carry bracket legs. Fractional and notional orders cannot use OPG at all ([Alpaca: Orders](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/orders-at-alpaca)). Alpaca staff state that paper does not implement OPG or CLS. Paper fills them at the current quote with random partial fills and cancels them 30 seconds after the open; on one day **59.7% of paper OPG orders were cancelled** against a normal rate of about 1% ([Alpaca forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/recently-seeing-partial-fills-on-market-opg-order-s-p500-stocks/13906)). Paper also does not distinguish market-on-open from market orders ([Alpaca forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/opening-and-closing-market-prices-in-paper-trading/11664)). Live market-on-open orders fill at the primary auction price, which can differ from a vendor's "open" ([Alpaca forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/market-on-open-order-gives-me-a-worse-price-than-daily-open/14856)). There is a documentation conflict. The order docs mark OPG as "Yes*" with a "contact sales" note, while forum users report placing OPG orders as retail customers. One live test order would settle it.

The direction of the evidence on cost at the open is consistent, even though the evidence itself is dated. Volatility is concentrated in the first two minutes after the open ([CFS working paper](https://gfk-cfs.de/media//08_45.pdf)), and the first trade's effective spread is lower when the open is a call auction ([Darden working paper](https://www.darden.virginia.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/Open_2001_WP.pdf)). No 2020-2026 study gave bps costs by minute for S&P 500 names, so Frozen_stock's own fills, bucketed by time of day, are the best evidence available. For a few hundred to a few thousand dollars, a market-on-open order adds no meaningful imbalance and pays no spread. It is probably the cheapest way to get the "next open" price backtests assume, but that is an inference to test, not a finding.

### Live retail costs vary widely by broker and must be measured

Alpaca receives payment for order flow and routes non-directed orders to Virtu, Citadel Securities, Jane Street and GTS ([Alpaca 606 2026Q2](https://files.alpaca.markets/disclosures/library/SEC+606a1+-+2026Q2.pdf)). FINRA cited deficiencies in its Rule 606 disclosures in 2025 ([FINRA SD-2436](https://finra.org/sites/default/files/2025-03/SD-2436-Alpaca-03-06-2025.pdf)). The best recent primary estimate of retail execution cost comes from 85,000 simultaneous market orders across five brokers. Mean account-level round-trip costs ranged from **7 to 46 bps**, driven by wholesalers pricing different brokers differently rather than by payment-for-order-flow levels ([Schwarz et al., JF 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v80y2025i5p2507-2541.html)). Secondary summaries disagree on units, so read the paper before quoting exact figures ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/actual-stock-trading-frictions-by-broker)). Paper fills at the NBBO with no price improvement, no queue modelling and no size check ([Alpaca: Paper Trading](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/paper-trading)). Paper therefore overstates the cost of small continuous-market orders and understates size and latency costs. Auction orders bypass wholesalers, so price improvement does not apply to them.

### IEX bars are a 3% sample of the tape, and stops should key off the consolidated tape

IEX carried about **3.2% of total US equity volume** in Q4 2025 ([IEX news](https://iex.io/news)). On one day Alpaca's own example shows AAPL trading 923,134 shares on IEX against 51.9 million across all venues ([Alpaca FAQ](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/market-data-faq)). IEX opens, highs and lows therefore differ from consolidated ones, and some intervals have no IEX bar. The free plan can still query historical SIP bars whose end time is at least 15 minutes old ([Alpaca FAQ](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/market-data-faq)). Signals, backtests and slippage benchmarks can therefore all use consolidated data at no cost. Broker-side sell stops elect only on a consolidated-tape trade at or below the stop and within the NBBO. Once elected they become market orders and fill through gaps, while stop-limits can sit unfilled below a gap ([Alpaca: Orders](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/orders-at-alpaca)). App-side stops driven by IEX trades can fire late, never, or on thin IEX-only prints. Broker-side stops match SIP-based backtests better and should be the primary protection.

### Experiments on execution

Two experiments can run today. The first is feed and benchmark hygiene. Fetch delayed SIP daily and minute bars after 4:15 p.m. ET, confirm that signals and backtests use the same feed, and log daily how often IEX highs and lows miss SIP stop or target touches. Re-benchmark every logged fill against the official SIP open or the 9:30 SIP minute bar. Tag fills as paper or live and auction or continuous, and exclude paper OPG fills from the cost calibrator, which would otherwise learn the simulator rather than the market. Check that backtests fill gapped stops at min(stop, open), not at the stop. The second is a **limit-on-open gap filter** backtested on daily bars. A limit-on-open priced at prior close ± k·ATR either fills at the cross price or skips the trade, so it acts as a signal filter, not a cost saving. Score the "missed winners" from skipped gap-downs separately for mean-reversion and breakout strategies.

A third experiment needs a new but free, backfillable data pull: compare entries at 9:45 and 10:00 against the open using delayed SIP minute bars, measuring both spread cost and signal drift. The remaining tests need live, small-size orders, because paper cannot answer them. Randomly assign each entry to market-on-open or a 9:30:05 market order and benchmark both against the official open. Compare a marketable-limit collar (ask + 5-10 bps) with a plain market order. After OPG fills, compare an OCO with a stop-market leg against one with a stop-limit leg, measuring the unprotected seconds between auction fill and OCO acceptance. A separate market-structure watch item: the half-penny tick and access-fee changes have been delayed, possibly to November 2027 ([MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/pdf/resources/insights/260612-sec-proposes-landmark-rollback-of-core-regulation)). If they take effect, mega-cap spreads narrow and the cost calibration should be refreshed.

## Slots, not symbols, cap the evidence rate

### Correlated trades are worth far less than their count

The effective number of independent trades follows the Kish design effect, n_eff = n / (1 + (m − 1)ρ), where m is the number of trades sharing a cluster and ρ their within-cluster correlation. In Kish's worked example, 600 observations in 30 clusters with ρ = 0.10 carry the information of about 207 ([DMAR design_effect](https://www.rdocumentation.org/packages/DMAR/versions/1.0.0/topics/design_effect)). Petersen shows OLS standard errors understate the truth when both regressor and residual are correlated within a cluster, by 30% in one of his simulations. Date-clustered errors fix this "only when there are a sufficient number of clusters"; with about 10 clusters, false rejections at the 1% level rise to 5% ([Petersen 2009](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/u.osu.edu/dist/7/36891/files/2020/03/PetersenRFS2009clusteringfixedeffectspaneldata.pdf)). For dependent bets, effective breadth "can be much much less than the number of assets", with beta and sector exposure as the main culprits ([QuantRocket lecture](https://www.quantrocket.com/codeload/quant-finance-lectures/quant_finance_lectures/Lecture39-Why-Hedge.ipynb)).

Average implied correlation among the 50 largest S&P 500 stocks has averaged about **37% since 2006** ([Acadian](https://www.acadian-asset.com/investment-insights/owenomics/the-frenzied-serenity-of-the-stock-market)). It fell below 10 in April 2024 ([Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2024/07/01/big-names-s-p-500-overshadow-risk-volatility-jump)), and S&P 500 correlations hit records in March 2020 ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/en/research-insights/articles/coronaviral-correlations)). On raw returns, N_eff tends to 1/ρ, so 45, 150 and 500 names each carry only about 4-7 independent bets a day in calm markets and about 2 in stress (in-house arithmetic). That is why a day of clustered stops carries the evidence of one or two trades, not ten. On market-residual returns, with an assumed ρ of 0.01-0.05, the cap rises to 20-100, and breadth finally pays. The current 45 names also include index and sector ETFs that nearly duplicate the megacaps, so the book is narrower than its headline count.

### Widening to liquid S&P 500 names should hold per-trade edge if spreads are screened

The evidence suggests per-trade expectancy does not fall when the universe widens within large caps, provided the spread is controlled. The largest short-run reversals are in high-turnover, illiquid stocks, where profits fall below likely costs ([Avramov, Chordia and Goyal](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v61y2006i5p2365-2394.html)). Restricting to large caps cuts the cost drag significantly ([de Groot, Huij and Zhou](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jbfina:v:36:y:2012:i:2:p:371-382)). Residual reversal remains profitable net of costs even in large stocks ([Robeco](https://www.robeco.com/en-uk/insights/2022/05/beyond-fama-french-alpha-from-short-term-signals)). Momentum profits "exhibit no reliable relation with size" ([Israel and Moskowitz](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v108y2013i2p275-301.html)), though that is 12-month momentum, not daily breakouts. Moving from 45 megacaps to the 200-300 most liquid point-in-time S&P 500 names probably raises gross reversal edge slightly while keeping spreads near megacap levels. Moving further into the full 500 or the Russell 1000 tail adds trades mainly in wider-spread names.

Throughput is the binding constraint. Closed trades per week per strategy are at most slots divided by average hold. With 5 slots and a 5-session hold, a strategy closes at most about 5 trades a week however many symbols it watches. The in-house illustration below assumes 0.8 trades a week per strategy at 45 names, linear signal scaling and a target of 30 effective trades. The point-in-time replay should recompute it from real signal counts.

| Universe | Trades/wk/strategy | Weeks to 30 effective (raw ρ 0.30) | Weeks to 30 effective (residual ρ 0.05) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 45 (current) | 0.8 | ~37 | ~37 |
| 150 | 2.7 | ~12 | ~11 |
| 500, 5-slot cap | 5.0 (capped) | ~9 | ~7 |
| 500, exploration slots raised | 8.9 | ~7 | ~4 |
| 250 liquid subset, slots raised | ~4.4 | ~10 | ~7 |

The breadth that maximises learning speed without degrading expectancy is therefore not a symbol count. It is the liquid point-in-time S&P 500 subset in the 0.25%-per-trade exploration lane, with more exploration slots (40 slots × 0.25% is only 10% gross exposure), per-date and per-sector entry caps that bound m, and the main lane left at 45 names until verdicts arrive. Two caveats bind. Stress weeks bunch signals onto one date at high ρ, roughly halving the evidence per trade. And the one-share floor makes a $400 stock require about $160,000 of equity at 0.25% sizing, so names where one share exceeds about twice the target size must be skipped and logged, not silently dropped.

### Experiments on breadth

All the breadth experiments start on existing data. The first estimates ρ from the bot's own closed trades: compute raw and SPY-residual (and sector-ETF-residual) returns, then the intraclass correlation by entry date. Report date-clustered t-statistics and a one-observation-per-signal-date t-test next to the current verdicts. This also feeds the variance inputs for the e-process judge in the first section. The second counts signals per week per strategy on the point-in-time S&P 500 replay by liquidity tercile (median dollar volume, a spread proxy, price above about $10). Compare net-of-spread expectancy across terciles to find where edge starts to degrade. The cut-off values are practitioner-style suggestions with no primary source. The third re-tests the existing VIX, ADX and SPY-trend gates on the wider universe, because those gates condition on exactly the high-correlation states where signals bunch. The live step, which needs only configuration, opens the exploration lane to the liquid subset with raised slot caps and a cap of about 3 new entries per strategy per date. Verdicts should then require both 30 effective trades and 30 distinct signal dates.

## Every experiment ranked by expected value

The ranking weighs how much each experiment changes the bot's decisions against its cost. Experiments that fix how evidence is judged come first because they change the reading of every later result. Data status is "existing" when the bot's trade logs, yfinance bars, point-in-time S&P 500 replay, VIX/FRED series or Fed calendar suffice. It is "backfillable" when a free one-off download gives history immediately, "forward" when history must accumulate before testing, and "live" when only real-money orders can answer the question.

PEAD in large caps, Heston-Sadka seasonality, unconditional pre-FOMC drift, classic [0,+3] turn-of-month, short-horizon breakouts on index products, 13F, Reddit and backfilled Google Trends should receive no further trials.

## Conclusion

The research reframes what "self-evolving" can mean at this trade rate. The engine cannot learn whether a 10-20 bps gate tweak helps, because no statistical method can deliver that verdict from a few trades a week. Treating 20-trade results as verdicts mostly rotates noise through production. The defensible goal is narrower and more useful: cut harmful changes quickly with bounded error, keep small-effect changes labelled provisional, and spend the false-adoption budget only where forward e-values cross a pre-committed online level. Seen this way, breadth and residual-return evaluation are not separate projects. They are the only levers that raise the rate of independent evidence enough for the stricter judge to reach verdicts in months rather than years.

The anomaly and data research points in the same direction. What survives in liquid large caps is idiosyncratic overshoot measured against sector or market, the very construction that lowers trade correlation and raises effective breadth. One design change, ranking and evaluating trades on sector- or market-relative returns, therefore serves profit, learning speed and statistical validity at once. Execution research is the area where the bot's current environment cannot answer the question: paper trading cannot test the auction. Until small live trading begins, execution work should be limited to clean benchmarks and honest calibration.
