# Universe breadth vs. rate of independent evidence for Frozen_stock

Scope: how widening the live universe from 45 megacaps/ETFs to 150-500 names changes (a) the effective number of independent closed trades, (b) per-trade expectancy after costs, and (c) time-to-verdict. Researched 2026-11-07. Where a number is my own arithmetic it is in "Inferences" with the assumptions stated.

## (a) How to measure the effective number of independent trades under cross-sectional and time correlation

### Takeaway
Use a design-effect correction: n_eff = n / (1 + (m - 1)·ρ), where m is the number of trades sharing a cluster (signal/entry date, or overlapping holding window) and ρ is the within-cluster correlation of trade outcomes. Equivalently, compute t-statistics with standard errors clustered by entry date (Petersen 2009), or collapse to one averaged observation per signal date (Fama-MacBeth style). Measure ρ on market- or sector-residual trade returns, not raw P&L, because raw long-only P&L is dominated by one shared market bet.

### Cited Findings
- Kish design effect for equal clusters of size m with intraclass correlation ρ: DEFF = 1 + (m - 1)ρ; effective n = n / DEFF. Worked example: 30 clusters of 20 with ρ = 0.10 gives DEFF = 2.9 and an effective N of about 207 out of 600. For unequal clusters use the size-weighted mean m* = Σm²/Σm (Kish 1965, eq. 5.4). — [DMAR design_effect documentation](https://www.rdocumentation.org/packages/DMAR/versions/1.0.0/topics/design_effect)
- When clusters contain one unit each, DEFF = 1 regardless of ρ. — [DMAR design_effect documentation](https://www.rdocumentation.org/packages/DMAR/versions/1.0.0/topics/design_effect)
- Petersen (2009), RFS 22(1):435-480, eq. (7): with a fixed cluster effect, the true variance of an OLS slope is σ²_ε/(NT·σ²_X) · (1 + (T - 1)·ρ_X·ρ_ε), so OLS understates the standard error if and only if both the regressor and the residual are correlated within the cluster; the error grows with cluster length. In his simulation with T = 5 and ρ_X = ρ_ε = 0.5, OLS understated the true standard error by 30% (0.0283 vs 0.0406). — [Petersen 2009 (PDF)](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/u.osu.edu/dist/7/36891/files/2020/03/PetersenRFS2009clusteringfixedeffectspaneldata.pdf)
- Petersen: a "time effect" (residuals correlated across firms on the same date) is the relevant structure for equity returns; Fama-MacBeth and standard errors clustered by time are unbiased for it, "but only when there are a sufficient number of clusters"; with too few clusters, clustered SEs are biased even on the right dimension. In his simulations the share of t-stats above 2.58 rises from 1% to 5% when the number of clusters falls to 10. — [Petersen 2009 (PDF)](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/u.osu.edu/dist/7/36891/files/2020/03/PetersenRFS2009clusteringfixedeffectspaneldata.pdf)
- Petersen: with a temporary (decaying) effect, variance is σ²_ε/(NT·σ²_X) · [1 + (2/T)·Σ_k (T - k)·ρ_x,k·ρ_ε,k], i.e. a Newey-West-type sum over lagged autocorrelations; this is the form relevant for overlapping 1-20 session holds. — [Petersen 2009 (PDF)](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/u.osu.edu/dist/7/36891/files/2020/03/PetersenRFS2009clusteringfixedeffectspaneldata.pdf)
- Petersen found 42% of recent finance papers with panel regressions did not adjust standard errors for residual dependence. — [Petersen 2009 (PDF)](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/u.osu.edu/dist/7/36891/files/2020/03/PetersenRFS2009clusteringfixedeffectspaneldata.pdf)
- Grinold-Kahn's fundamental law (IR ≈ IC·√BR) lacks a measurable definition of breadth; Buckle (2004, Journal of Asset Management 4(6):393-405) derives a "semi-generalised" law of the same form with a measurable breadth. — [Buckle 2004, IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/assmgt/v4y2004i6d10.1057_palgrave.jam.2240118.html)
- Under independence breadth = number of assets × number of periods; when bets are dependent, effective breadth "can be much much less than the number of assets", and beta and sector exposure are named as the main sources of correlation among bets. — [QuantRocket lecture "Why Hedge" (Quantopian lecture series)](https://www.quantrocket.com/codeload/quant-finance-lectures/quant_finance_lectures/Lecture39-Why-Hedge.ipynb)
- Buckle's follow-up (2005) reports the remaining assumptions of the law are "surprisingly innocuous" for ex-post performance. — [Buckle 2005, IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/assmgt/v6y2005i1d10.1057_palgrave.jam.2240163.html)
- PCA-based "effective number of bets" (Meucci 2009): ENB = exp of the entropy of the portfolio's risk distribution across uncorrelated factors. The principal-components version was later abandoned for instability and non-uniqueness of PCs, and can give an ENB near 1 for an equal-weight equity portfolio; Meucci, Santangelo & Deguest (2014) replaced it with "minimum torsion" bets. — [Portfolio Optimizer blog on ENB](https://portfoliooptimizer.io/blog/the-effective-number-of-bets-measuring-portfolio-diversification/); [Deguest, Martellini & Meucci, "Risk Parity and Beyond" (PDF)](https://www.top1000funds.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/Risk-Parity-and-Beyond-From-Asset-Allocation-to-Risk-Allocation-Decisions.pdf)

### Inferences
- For N equally weighted bets with common pairwise correlation ρ, the Kish formula and the correlated-bets breadth are the same algebra: N_eff = N / (1 + (N - 1)ρ), which tends to 1/ρ as N grows. Adding names stops adding independent information once N >> 1/ρ.
- The marginal information from the m-th trade opened on the same date is small when ρ is high. Cumulative n_eff for m same-date trades: at ρ = 0.30 (raw long-only returns), m = 1, 2, 3, 5, 10 give 1.0, 1.54, 1.88, 2.27, 2.70. At ρ = 0.05 (market-residual returns, assumed), the same m give 1.0, 1.90, 2.73, 4.17, 6.90. So evaluating trades on residual returns (trade return minus β × SPY return over the same holding window, or minus the sector ETF) can roughly double or triple the effective count from clustered days. The edge is then measured as residual alpha, which is the part a strategy actually owns.
- A practical Frozen_stock estimator: (1) for each closed trade compute raw and residual return; (2) group by entry date (or by overlapping holding window); (3) report (i) a t-stat with SEs clustered by entry date, (ii) a "one observation per signal date" t-test (mean of the date-cluster means), and (iii) n_eff = n/DEFF with ρ estimated as the intraclass correlation of residual returns. Petersen's few-clusters result means a verdict needs at least about 30-50 distinct signal dates, not just 30 trades. With fewer than about 10-20 date clusters, no clustered SE is reliable.
- The "~20-30 closed trades" verdict rule has little statistical power even with independent trades. Detecting a mean edge at t ≈ 2 needs n_eff ≈ (2/S)², where S is per-trade Sharpe (mean/sd of trade return). S = 0.36 needs 30; S = 0.2 needs 100; S = 0.1 needs 400. With a 5-session large-cap trade sd of about 3.5-4.5% (assuming 1.5-2% daily vol), 30 trades detects only mean edges above roughly 1.3-1.6% per trade. Realistic short-horizon edges (tens of bp) need hundreds of effective trades, which is the strongest argument for breadth.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve Buckle's exact breadth formula (paywalled), or Clarke, de Silva & Thorley (2002 FAJ; 2006) on transfer coefficient and correlated breadth. Their expression "BR_eff ≈ N/(1+(N-1)ρ)" for equicorrelated signals is standard but is not verified from the primary text here.
- No source found that directly estimates ρ for residual trade outcomes of daily long-only strategies. The 0.05 figure is an assumption to be measured from Frozen_stock's own trade log.

## (b) How effective breadth grows with universe size for typical US large-cap correlations

### Takeaway
Average pairwise correlation among large US stocks moves from about 0.1-0.25 in calm markets to well above 0.45 in stress (record highs in March 2020). With raw returns, N_eff is capped near 1/ρ, so 45, 150 and 500 names all give only about 4-7 independent bets in calm markets and about 2 in stress. Breadth only pays off once the market (and sector) component is removed. The 45-name book is also much narrower than it looks, because index and sector ETFs are near-duplicates of the megacaps.

### Cited Findings
- Cboe implied correlation indices (COR1M, COR3M, COR6M, COR9M, COR1Y) measure the average correlation among the top 50 value-weighted S&P 500 components, using ATM index and single-stock implied vols in a Markowitz variance framework. — [Cboe COR3M launch release (2021)](https://ir.cboe.com/news/news-details/2021/Cboe-Announces-Launch-of-New-Cboe-3-Month-Implied-Correlation-Index-07-01-2021/default.aspx); [Cboe suite expansion (2022)](https://ir.cboe.com/news/news-details/2022/Cboe-Expands-Implied-Correlation-Index-Suite-Adds-Eight-Additional-Volatility-Related-Indices-07-18-2022/default.aspx); [Cboe Implied Correlation white paper](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/indices/documents/Implied_Correlation-WhitePaper-v1.0.5.pdf)
- COR1M averaged about 37% since 2006, and a reading near 8% was in the bottom 1% of its history (date of the reading not clear from the source). — [Acadian, "The frenzied serenity of the stock market"](https://www.acadian-asset.com/investment-insights/owenomics/the-frenzied-serenity-of-the-stock-market)
- COR3M touched a record low of 17.59 in July 2023. S&P DJI's 30-day realized correlation was 0.22 at end-June 2023, near its lowest since November 2021. — [Reuters via Metrobank, "Out-of-sync US stocks hide market risks"](https://wealthinsights.metrobank.com.ph/reuters/analysis-out-of-sync-u-s-stocks-hide-market-risks)
- In 2024 the Cboe implied-correlation gauge for the 50 largest S&P 500 stocks was below 10 at the start of April, a level rarely reached in its roughly two-decade history. — [Advisor Perspectives / Bloomberg, July 2024](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2024/07/01/big-names-s-p-500-overshadow-risk-volatility-jump)
- S&P DJI: correlations within the S&P 500 reached record levels for the month ended March 20, 2020. Its analysis treats correlation above 0.45 as "elevated" (11 such high-dispersion/high-correlation months since 1971). — [S&P Global, "Coronaviral correlations"](https://www.spglobal.com/en/research-insights/articles/coronaviral-correlations) (403 on fetch; figures from search snippet); [S&P DJI dispersion/volatility/correlation dashboard](https://spglobal.com/spdji/zh/documents/performance-reports/Dashboard-dispersion-volatility-correlation.pdf)
- Stock correlations rise as markets decline. — [Yahoo Finance/Reuters, "Stock correlations tick up as Wall Street extends declines"](https://sg.finance.yahoo.com/news/stock-correlations-tick-wall-street-202744443.html)

### Inferences
- N_eff = N/(1+(N-1)ρ) on raw daily returns (my arithmetic):

| ρ (regime) | 45 names | 150 names | 500 names | cap 1/ρ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.15 (calm, 2023-24-like) | 5.9 | 6.4 | 6.6 | 6.7 |
| 0.25 (normal) | 3.8 | 3.9 | 4.0 | 4 |
| 0.50 (stress) | 2.0 | 2.0 | 2.0 | 2 |
| 0.03 (residual, assumed) | 19.4 | 27.4 | 31.3 | 33 |
| 0.01 (residual, assumed) | 31.3 | 60.2 | 83.5 | 100 |

- On raw returns, going from 45 to 500 names adds almost nothing per date. Breadth adds independent evidence mainly through (i) more distinct signal dates over time, and (ii) residual-return evaluation, where the cap 1/ρ is large.
- Caveat: the Cboe measures cover only the top 50 megacaps. The broader S&P 500 typically has lower average pairwise correlation (more idiosyncratic mid-caps), so the real gain from 45 to 500 names is somewhat better than the equicorrelation table suggests. The current 45 names also include SPY/QQQ-type index ETFs and sector ETFs that are highly correlated with each other and with the megacaps. Their effective count is probably well under the 45 headline, possibly near the 25-30 single names plus about 1-2 for the ETF block (assumption, not measured).
- Frozen_stock's "many stops on one day" observation is what the stress row predicts: when ρ jumps toward 0.5, every open long is roughly one bet, so a day's stop cluster carries the evidence of about 1-2 trades, not 5-10.

### Gaps
- No primary series found for long-run average realized pairwise correlation of all S&P 500 members (the widely quoted "~0.3 normal / 0.7-0.8 crisis" could not be verified). The S&P DJI dashboard PDF holds percentile bands but was not readable here.
- No source quantifying residual (beta/sector-adjusted) pairwise correlation for S&P 500 stocks; it should be estimated in-house from the point-in-time S&P 500 backtest data.

## (c) Do reversal, pullback/mean-reversion and breakout/momentum edges strengthen or weaken from megacaps to S&P 500 / Russell 1000 after costs?

### Takeaway
Short-term reversal is gross-strongest in small, illiquid, high-turnover stocks, but costs eat most of that. Within large caps a smaller edge can survive costs, especially when measured within industry or on residual returns. The edge is strongly state-dependent: largest when VIX is high and after market drops. Momentum profits show no reliable relation with size. Moving from megacaps to S&P 500 / top-300-by-liquidity is defensible; going to Russell 1000's tail or small caps raises gross edge but probably not net edge at daily turnover.

### Cited Findings
- Avramov, Chordia & Goyal (2006, JF 61(5):2365-2394): the largest short-run reversals are in high-turnover, low-liquidity stocks; contrarian profits are smaller than likely transaction costs; reversal is related to illiquidity even after controlling for volume. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v61y2006i5p2365-2394.html); [NBER conference draft](https://users.nber.org/~confer/2004/mmsu04/goyal.pdf)
- de Groot, Huij & Zhou (2012, JBF 36(2):371-382): reversal strategies earn 30-50 bp per week net of trading costs; the cost drag "can largely be attributed to excessively trading in small cap stocks"; limiting to large caps significantly reduces costs, and lower-turnover construction reduces them further. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jbfina:v:36:y:2012:i:2:p:371-382); [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/short-term-reversal-in-stocks)
- CXO Advisory summary of the same paper (sample 1990-2009): with small caps included, reasonable frictions flip the result to a net weekly loss; weekly rather than daily rebalancing cuts friction but degrades gross reversal returns. — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=6251)
- Blitz, Huij and coauthors: residual reversal strategies are significantly profitable net of costs even when restricted to large stocks. — [Robeco, "Beyond Fama-French: alpha from short-term signals"](https://www.robeco.com/en-uk/insights/2022/05/beyond-fama-french-alpha-from-short-term-signals) (as summarized in search results)
- Cheng, Hameed, Subrahmanyam & Titman (2014): except for micro-caps, the standard Jegadeesh reversal strategy earns insignificant returns on average in the more recent period. This conflicts with de Groot et al.'s large-cap net profits; the difference is likely raw vs residual/industry-adjusted construction and sample period. — [Cheng et al., "Short-term reversals and the efficiency of liquidity provision" (ABFER 2014 PDF)](https://www.abfer.org/media/abfer-events-2014/annual-conference/investment-finance/short-term-reversals-and-the-efficiency-of-liquidity-provision.pdf)
- Nagel (2012, RFS 25(7):2005-2039): reversal returns proxy liquidity-provision returns. Expected returns are strongly time-varying and predictable by VIX, and returns and conditional Sharpe ratios "increase enormously" in turmoil (2007-09). Industry-portfolio reversals earn little on average but high returns when VIX is high. — [NBER w17653](https://nber.org/papers/w17653); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:25:y:2012:i:7:p:2005-2039)
- Hameed & Mian (2015, JFQA 50:89-117): intra-industry monthly reversals are larger than conventional reversals, persist over time, and are significant even among large-cap, liquid, high-institutional-ownership stocks. They are stronger in stocks with more frictions, and stronger after aggregate market declines and in volatile times. A 2010 draft (NYSE/AMEX 1963-2006) reported 1.46%/month within 24 industries vs 1.05% conventional, gross of costs. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v50y2015i1-2p89-117_00.html); [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/short-term-reversal-by-industry)
- Da, Liu & Schaumburg (2014, Management Science 60(3):658-674): removing the cash-flow-news component (via analyst revisions) gives a residual reversal strategy with about 4x the risk-adjusted return of the standard strategy. On the long side, reversal is driven by liquidity shocks from forced sellers. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:60:y:2014:i:3:p:658-674); [NY Fed Staff Report 513](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/staff_reports/sr513.html)
- Da & Schaumburg (2008): short-run reversal among S&P 500 stocks is significantly enhanced by conditioning on analyst target prices and is linked to stock-specific liquidity events. — [Kellogg, "Short run reversal in the market for large stocks"](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2008/short-run-reversal-in-the-market-for-large-stocks/?p=1)
- Israel & Moskowitz (2013, JFE 108(2):275-301): momentum profits "exhibit no reliable relation with size" (the value premium weakens with size); long positions account for about half of momentum profits; little evidence that changes in trading costs explain the returns. Results cover 86 years of US data. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v108y2013i2p275-301.html); [AQR](https://www.aqr.com/library/journal-articles/the-role-of-shorting-firm-size-and-time-on-market-anomalies)

### Inferences
- For Frozen_stock's RSI(2)/IBS/pullback strategies (liquidity-provision trades), widening from megacaps to the S&P 500 likely raises gross per-trade edge somewhat (less liquid names reverse more), while retail-sized orders keep impact low. The binding cost is the bid-ask spread, which widens as you go down the cap/liquidity spectrum. A liquidity screen should keep the net edge from degrading.
- Hameed-Mian and Da-Liu-Schaumburg suggest a selection method that should beat plain breadth: rank pullback candidates on industry-relative (or sector-ETF-relative) short-term return rather than raw return. Signals then target idiosyncratic overshoots, which are both more profitable and less correlated across trades. This lowers ρ in the design effect.
- Nagel and Hameed-Mian imply mean-reversion signals and their payoffs cluster after market drops and high VIX, the same states where cross-sectional correlation spikes. So a wider universe will produce bursts of many same-day signals, with low information per trade (high ρ) but historically high payoff. Per-date caps plus residual-return evaluation handle this. The existing VIX/ADX/SPY-trend gate findings should be re-tested on the wider universe because the gates condition on exactly this state.
- Breakout/momentum (Channel Breakout): Israel-Moskowitz suggests size per se is not a reason to expect a weaker edge in a wider large-cap universe. Their evidence is for 12-1 month momentum, not daily breakouts.

### Gaps
- No source found on ETF vs single-stock short-term mean reversion (e.g., sector ETF RSI(2) vs constituent RSI(2)) net of costs.
- No source found on daily breakout/channel strategies by size bucket after costs.
- de Groot et al.'s exact universe and cost model, and the recent-period (post-2015) large-cap net reversal profitability, were not verified from full text.

## (d) Practical recommendation: breadth, selection, survivorship/liquidity handling, allocation and time-to-verdict

### Takeaway
Widen the exploration lane (0.25% equity per trade) to the point-in-time S&P 500 filtered to about the 200-300 most liquid names, keep the main lane at 45 until verdicts arrive, evaluate on residual returns with date-clustered statistics, cap same-day trades per strategy, and raise exploration slot caps. Slots, not universe size, are what limit evidence. Under illustrative assumptions this cuts time-to-30-effective-trades per strategy from about 9 months to about 1-3 months.

### Cited Findings
- Clustered SEs need enough clusters; with only about 10 clusters, t-stat rejection rates double or worse. — [Petersen 2009 (PDF)](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/u.osu.edu/dist/7/36891/files/2020/03/PetersenRFS2009clusteringfixedeffectspaneldata.pdf)
- Cost drag on reversal strategies comes mainly from small-cap trading; large-cap restriction and lower turnover cut costs. — [de Groot, Huij & Zhou 2012](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jbfina:v:36:y:2012:i:2:p:371-382)
- Reversal profits in the least liquid stocks are smaller than likely costs. — [Avramov, Chordia & Goyal 2006](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v61y2006i5p2365-2394.html)

### Inferences
- Throughput is slot-bound, not universe-bound. Max closed trades per week per strategy ≈ slots / average hold (weeks). With 5 slots and a 5-session average hold, that is at most about 5 per week per strategy and 20 per week in total. Widening the universe raises trade count only until slots bind. Beyond that it improves choice (ranking can pick better or less correlated candidates) but not count. The 0.25% exploration lane is where slots can be raised cheaply: 40 exploration slots × 0.25% = 10% gross.
- Illustrative time-to-verdict per strategy (my arithmetic). Assumptions: about 0.8 closed trades/week/strategy at 45 names, signals scale linearly with universe size, trades fall on about 2 distinct signal dates per week, target = 30 effective trades, design effect applied per date:

| Universe | Trades/wk/strategy | n_eff/wk, raw ρ=0.30 | Weeks to 30 (raw) | n_eff/wk, residual ρ=0.05 | Weeks to 30 (residual) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 45 (current) | 0.8 | ~0.8 | ~37 | ~0.8 | ~37 |
| 150 | 2.7 | 2.4 | ~12 | 2.7 | ~11 |
| 500, 5-slot cap | 5.0 (capped) | 3.4 | ~9 | 4.5 | ~7 |
| 500, slots raised (exploration) | 8.9 | 4.4 | ~7 | 7.6 | ~4 |
| 250 liquid subset, slots raised | ~4.4 | 3.1 | ~10 | 4.1 | ~7 |

These figures assume calm markets. In stress weeks ρ is around 0.5 or higher and signals bunch onto 1 date, so evidence per trade roughly halves. Treat the table as orders of magnitude and recompute it from Frozen_stock's actual signal counts, which the point-in-time S&P 500 backtest can supply per strategy.
- Recommended breadth: point-in-time S&P 500 membership for the exploration lane, filtered to roughly the top 200-300 by 20-day median dollar volume. Likely screens: price above about $10, median dollar ADV well above position size (e.g. > $50-100M), and a spread proxy cap (e.g. median quoted spread < 5-10 bp). Exclude names with earnings within the hold window where the strategy is not earnings-aware. The threshold values are practitioner-style suggestions; I found no primary source for specific cut-offs. Expected gain is about 3-5x the trades per week of the current book, with costs close to megacap levels. Going to the full 500 or the Russell 1000 adds trades mostly in names with wider spreads and is worth testing only via backtest net-of-spread comparison by liquidity tercile.
- Selection method within the wider universe: (1) rank candidates by sector/industry-relative signal strength (Hameed-Mian, Da-Liu-Schaumburg); (2) cap new entries per strategy per date (e.g. 3) and per sector, to cap m in the design effect; (3) prefer candidates with low residual correlation to existing open positions.
- Evaluation changes: record each trade's residual return vs SPY (and vs sector ETF), and compute verdicts with entry-date-clustered SEs or date-averaged t-tests. Require both at least 30 effective trades and at least 30 distinct signal dates. Report raw and residual results, because the bot is paid on raw P&L.
- Allocation: at 0.25% of equity per trade, the one-share floor binds for high-priced names. A $400 stock needs at least $160k equity at 0.25% (my arithmetic), so for smaller accounts either allow fractional shares if the broker supports them, or skip names where 1 share exceeds about 2x the target size, logged as "skipped, floor" so it does not bias results. Main-lane position size is unchanged because the main lane keeps its 45 names.
- Survivorship: live trading has no survivorship bias, but the backtests used to estimate signal rates and net edge for the wider universe must use the point-in-time S&P 500 membership with delisted names' returns included. Megacap-only backtests overstate survivorship-free performance less than mid-cap backtests do, because mid-cap delistings and index deletions are more frequent. This is general practice; specific bias magnitudes were not researched here.

### Gaps
- No primary source found for standard practitioner liquidity cut-offs (min price, min ADV, max spread) for retail daily strategies.
- No source found estimating signals per week for RSI(2)/IBS/Bollinger/breakout rules as a function of universe breadth. This needs an in-house backtest count.
- Survivorship-bias magnitudes for S&P 500-wide short-horizon strategies, and delisting-return handling, were not researched in this pass.
- Clarke, de Silva & Thorley (transfer coefficient, constraint effects on effective breadth for long-only portfolios) were not retrieved. Their result that long-only constraints sharply reduce the transfer coefficient is relevant but unverified here.
