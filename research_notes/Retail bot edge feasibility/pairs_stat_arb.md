# Market-neutral pairs trading and statistical arbitrage in US large caps and ETFs (post-2015, retail scale)

Scope note: this covers distance, cointegration and copula pairs, PCA/ETF residual stat-arb, ML stat-arb, ETF and share-class pairs, regime dependence, retail frictions on Alpaca, evidence rate, and a build recommendation for Frozen_stock's paper short lane. Researched 2026-11-07. Several PDFs were read in full (Rad/Low/Faff 2016 accepted manuscript, Krauss/Do/Huck 2016 working paper, Zhu 2024, Yang & Chen 2025, Sun 2025). Others were read only as abstracts or secondary summaries, and are marked as such.

## 1. Distance, cointegration and copula pairs: decay, and post-2015 large-cap results

### Takeaway
The classic distance-method (GGR) edge in US stocks peaked around the 1980s and has decayed steadily. After costs it was largely unprofitable after 2002 (Do & Faff 2012). From 2009, trading opportunities for distance and cointegration pairs fell sharply (Rad, Low & Faff 2016). The few post-2015 large-cap results that exist show thin positive net returns: about 0.3-0.4% a month with annualized Sharpe around 0.7-1.0 for clustered S&P 500 pairs, and less for the most liquid stocks. None of these is a strong peer-reviewed confirmation that a retail daily pairs book clears costs after 2015.

### Cited Findings
- Rad, Low & Faff (Quantitative Finance 2016), whole US market 1962-2014, time-varying costs. Mean monthly excess return is 91 / 85 / 43 bps before costs and 38 / 33 / 5 bps after costs for the distance, cointegration and copula methods. "From 2009, the frequency of trading opportunities via the distance and cointegration methods is reduced considerably," while it stayed stable for copula — [Rad, Low & Faff 2016 (accepted manuscript)](https://pure.bond.edu.au/ws/portalfiles/portal/36339487/AM_The_profitability_of_pairs_trading_strategies.pdf)
- Rad, Low & Faff cost model: institutional commissions falling from 70 bps (1962) to 9 bps (recent years), plus market impact of 30 bps (1962-1988) and 20 bps (1989 on). These are doubled because a pair trade is two round trips. No explicit short-borrow cost, because illiquid and small stocks are screened out — [Rad, Low & Faff 2016](https://pure.bond.edu.au/ws/portalfiles/portal/36339487/AM_The_profitability_of_pairs_trading_strategies.pdf)
- Rad, Low & Faff: the 5-year rolling Sharpe of all three methods rises until about 1985 and then declines. In recent years the three methods perform about the same, with "no clear winner." All three keep significant alphas after Fama-French 5-factor plus momentum and liquidity controls (except copula after costs), and show no correlation with market excess returns — [Rad, Low & Faff 2016](https://pure.bond.edu.au/ws/portalfiles/portal/36339487/AM_The_profitability_of_pairs_trading_strategies.pdf)
- Do & Faff (2010), as summarized by Rad, Low & Faff: distance-method performance peaks in the 1970s-80s and declines from the 1990s. The exceptions are the 2000-2002 and 2007-2009 bear markets. The decline is linked to a rising share of pairs that diverge and never converge — [Rad, Low & Faff 2016](https://pure.bond.edu.au/ws/portalfiles/portal/36339487/AM_The_profitability_of_pairs_trading_strategies.pdf)
- Do & Faff (2012), as summarized by Rad, Low & Faff: after time-varying trading costs the distance method is on average not profitable, and "largely unprofitable after 2002." Only the top 4 of 29 portfolios earn about 28 bps a month (3.37% a year) — [Rad, Low & Faff 2016](https://pure.bond.edu.au/ws/portalfiles/portal/36339487/AM_The_profitability_of_pairs_trading_strategies.pdf)
- Jacobs & Weber (2015), as summarized by Rad, Low & Faff: the distance method across 34 countries is positive but "immensely time-varying," which they attribute to investor under- and over-reaction to news — [Rad, Low & Faff 2016](https://pure.bond.edu.au/ws/portalfiles/portal/36339487/AM_The_profitability_of_pairs_trading_strategies.pdf)
- Zhu (2024), a Yale economics senior essay advised by Goetzmann (low evidentiary weight), replicates GGR on June 2004 to June 2023. Top-20 pairs earn 0.461% a month (t = 3.65, Sharpe 0.81), against 0.52% in GGR's Panel B. Top-500 pairs earn 0.498% a month (Sharpe 1.35). Restricting to liquid stocks lowers returns: the top 50% by volume earns 0.281% a month (Sharpe 0.77), and the top 25% earns only 0.188% a month (Sharpe 0.56). The returns use GGR's wait-one-day convention, and I found no explicit cost deduction in the methodology — [Zhu 2024](https://economics.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2024-05/Zhu_Pairs_Trading.pdf)
- Zhu: a one-standard-deviation rise in the momentum factor "can nearly wipe out the return" of the best strategy. The strategy loads on the short-term reversal factor, as in GGR and Jacobs & Weber — [Zhu 2024](https://economics.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2024-05/Zhu_Pairs_Trading.pdf)
- Fil (arXiv 2020), US equities 1990-2020 including Covid: the common distance and cointegration methods fail to beat the market benchmark even after hyperparameter tuning, but perform "very strongly during bear markets." The abstract says profitability "has dropped significantly in recent periods" — [Fil 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01157)
- Rotondi & Russo (2024), S&P 500 constituents 2000-2023, clustering-based pair selection with a partial-correlation distance: 36-41 bps a month excess return net of transaction costs, annualized Sharpe 0.72-0.99. This comes from a secondary literature summary; the primary paper was not read — [Sun 2025, Univ. of Warsaw WP 21/2025, lit. review](https://www.wne.uw.edu.pl/application/files/8917/5759/3293/WNE_WP484.pdf)
- Han, He & Toh (2023), clustering on firm-characteristic and return features across the US, UK and Hong Kong: the agglomerative-clustering strategy reports 24.8% annualized and Sharpe 2.69, against about 1.44 Sharpe for price-only clustering. Secondary summary; cost treatment and the post-2015 split not verified — [Sun 2025](https://www.wne.uw.edu.pl/application/files/8917/5759/3293/WNE_WP484.pdf)

### Inferences
- The most relevant post-2015 large-cap figure (Rotondi & Russo, about 0.4% a month net, Sharpe below 1) is about the size of the Do & Faff "best portfolio" result. It is an institutional-cost-style figure from a curated selection method, not a simple GGR replication.
- Zhu's liquidity gradient matters most here. The more liquid the universe, the smaller the gross return: about 2.3% a year gross for the top-quartile-volume stocks. A large-cap-only book should expect gross returns at the low end, with the cost advantage of large caps as the offset.
- Pairs returns load on short-term reversal. Frozen_stock's long-only sector-relative reversal already failed its gate after costs. A market-neutral pairs lane is largely the same signal with the market and sector beta hedged out. It may succeed where the long-only version failed only if the failure came from beta noise and not from a lack of residual edge. That is testable: compare the long-only reversal trades' sector-hedged residual P&L.

### Gaps
- Primary texts of Do & Faff (FAJ 2010; J. Financial Research 2012), Krauss (2017, J. Economic Surveys survey), Jacobs & Weber (2015), Chen et al. (2019, Management Science) and Rotondi & Russo (2024) were not read directly. Their figures here come from summaries in Rad, Low & Faff and Sun.
- I found no peer-reviewed study that isolates 2015-2026, S&P 500-only, distance or cointegration pairs at retail costs (near-zero commission, a few bps of spread). Fil (2020) gives no numbers in the abstract.

## 2. PCA / residual stat-arb (Avellaneda & Lee) and ML stat-arb, net of costs, at daily frequency in liquid stocks

### Takeaway
Residual mean reversion in liquid large caps still has gross structure: ML models report out-of-sample Sharpe around 3-4. Realistic daily turnover costs remove most of it. Guijarro-Ordonez, Pelger & Zanotti's Sharpe of about 4 drops to about 1 (roughly 5-6% a year) once 5 bps a trade and 1 bp a day short cost are charged, on data ending in 2016. Krauss, Do & Huck's daily S&P 500 ML stat-arb turns sharply negative after 5 bps per half-turn in 2010-2015, despite positive gross returns. Daily stat-arb survives only with turnover control: wider bands, longer holds, netting, and a cost-aware objective.

### Cited Findings
- Avellaneda & Lee (Quantitative Finance 2010): PCA-based market-neutral strategies earn an average annual Sharpe of 1.44 after costs over 1997-2007, falling to 0.9 for 2003-2007. ETF (sector-residual) strategies earn Sharpe 1.1 over 1997-2007, with similar degradation since 2002. Adding trading volume to ETF signals raises the 2003-2007 Sharpe to 1.51. The paper also links the August 2007 drawdown to an "unwinding" of quant funds — [Avellaneda & Lee 2010 (RePEc)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v10y2010i7p761-782.html); [working paper](https://math.nyu.edu/~avellane/AvellanedaLeeStatArb071008.pdf)
- Guijarro-Ordonez, Pelger & Zanotti ("Deep Learning Statistical Arbitrage"). Universe: daily CRSP stocks with market cap above 0.01% of the total, about the largest 550 (roughly the S&P 500). Out-of-sample trading runs January 2002 to December 2016, daily at the close. Without frictions, the CNN+Transformer reaches Sharpe 4.16 (8.7% mean, 2.1% vol) on IPCA residuals, 3.36 on PCA and 3.21 on Fama-French residuals. The parametric OU+threshold benchmark (Avellaneda-Lee-like) reaches only 0.97 (IPCA) and 0.73 (PCA) — [GOZ, arXiv 2106.04028 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.04028)
- GOZ frictions (Section III.J): cost = 5 bps × the L1 change in weights, plus 1 bp × the L1 short weights per day. Trained with this cost-aware objective, the net CNN+Transformer Sharpe is 0.94-1.24 for a reasonable number of IPCA factors (for example K=3: SR 1.24, mean 6.6%, vol 5.4%; K=5: SR 1.11, mean 5.5%). The friction-aware model trades less, is more active in 2007-2009 and less active in the "calm 2011-2015 bull market." The authors call these results a "lower bound" — [GOZ (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.04028)
- GOZ: about half the Sharpe ratio can persist for a one-week holding period, and arbitrageurs correct most mispricing within about a month — [GOZ (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.04028)
- Krauss, Do & Huck (EJOR 2017; 2016 working paper), S&P 500 survivor-bias-free, 1992-Oct 2015, daily long/short top and bottom k=10, costs of 5 bps per share per half-turn (following Avellaneda & Lee). Over the full sample the ensemble earns 0.45% a day before costs and 0.25% a day after. By sub-period, the ensemble Sharpe after costs is 6.69 in 12/92-03/01, 0.51 in 04/01-08/08 and 4.46 in 09/08-12/09. In 01/10-10/15 it earns 35.8% a year gross (Sharpe 1.75) but −18.0% a year after costs (Sharpe −0.88). Every model is negative after costs in 2010-2015 (−14% to −25% a year) — [Krauss, Do & Huck working paper](https://www.iwf.rw.fau.de/files/2016/03/03-2016.pdf); [EJOR record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:ejores:v:259:y:2017:i:2:p:689-702)
- Krauss, Do & Huck attribute the post-2010 decline to the public availability of ML methods and cheaper computing. Some structure remains (positive gross returns), "but the effects are not large enough to construct a profitable strategy" — [Krauss, Do & Huck working paper](https://www.iwf.rw.fau.de/files/2016/03/03-2016.pdf)
- Epstein (arXiv, October 2025), "Attention Factors for Statistical Arbitrage," largest US equities over a 24-year out-of-sample period: out-of-sample Sharpe above 4, and 2.3 "net of transaction costs" (abstract only; cost assumptions and post-2015 split not read) — [Epstein 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11616)
- Krause & Calliess (2024), as summarized by Sun: PCA+OU residual models reach Sharpe 0.87-0.96 with 3.9-4.6% annualized at about 4.5% vol, while an end-to-end autoencoder policy reaches Sharpe 1.42-1.81. Universe, period and cost treatment not verified — [Sun 2025](https://www.wne.uw.edu.pl/application/files/8917/5759/3293/WNE_WP484.pdf)
- A hobby replication of Avellaneda-Lee on 25 US stocks (2010-2018) reports the edge is too thin once 5 bps costs are included (non-peer-reviewed, small sample) — [GitHub replication](https://github.com/ayoubbenothman-ai/statistical-arbitrage-pca)

### Inferences
- The consistent pattern is gross Sharpe 3-4, net Sharpe about 1 or below once 5 bps per trade is charged, and negative net for naive daily rebalancing. At daily frequency, the cost model and turnover control decide the result more than the signal model does.
- Retail costs on mega caps may be below 5 bps a side: zero commission, and quoted spreads for the largest names are often around 1-2 bps (not sourced; measure from Alpaca fills). That could put a retail bot nearer the gross numbers than institutional backtests suggest. Paper fills, though, ignore impact and queue position (see section 5), so paper will flatter this.
- The simple OU+threshold residual model (the one a small bot can realistically build and audit) had out-of-sample Sharpe below 1 before costs in GOZ's 2002-2016 test. That is a sober prior for a simple build.

### Gaps
- GOZ's net-of-cost results for the PCA and OU benchmarks are in an appendix table I did not read. I found no 2017-2026 out-of-sample net-of-cost numbers for any of these methods.
- Epstein's cost assumptions and sub-period results were not read.

## 3. ETF pairs, ETF versus constituents, and share-class pairs

### Takeaway
Mechanically linked pairs are the most reliable mean reversion: SPY/IVV, GOOGL/GOOG. Their mispricings are tiny and short-lived (minutes), so they mostly belong to low-latency traders. The usable retail version is stock versus its sector ETF residual (Avellaneda-Lee "ETF method"), where the ETF hedge leg is always liquid and easy to borrow. GOOGL/GOOG has a 2025 paper claiming large model-implied profits, but it is a model calculation in a low-tier journal, not a walk-forward backtest.

### Cited Findings
- Marshall, Nguyen & Visaltanachoti, "ETF arbitrage: intraday evidence" (J. Banking & Finance 2013): SPY versus IVV (and versus IUSA) using cleaned high-frequency data, 2001-2010. Mispricings close fast and opportunities typically last about five minutes. SPY-IVV opportunities became sparse in the final years. Per-round-trip profits (about 0.3% for SPY-IVV, per the summary) are before broker fees — [RePEc record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jbfina:v:37:y:2013:i:9:p:3486-3498); [CXO Advisory summary](https://cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/etf-pairs-trading)
- Avellaneda & Lee's ETF-based strategy regresses each stock on its sector ETF and trades the residual. It earns Sharpe 1.1 over 1997-2007 after costs and 1.51 for 2003-2007 with volume-adjusted signals — [Avellaneda & Lee 2010](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v10y2010i7p761-782.html)
- Sun (2025), Chinese A-shares 2005-2024 (not US): synthetic-sector-ETF and PCA residual OU strategies each reach Sharpe about 0.90-0.95 after a 30 bps round-trip cost. ETF-based models are "stable during structural shifts," PCA models do better under high cross-sectional volatility, and volume adjustment helps the ETF models. Year by year, Sharpe was negative in 2017 and 2020 — [Sun 2025](https://www.wne.uw.edu.pl/application/files/8917/5759/3293/WNE_WP484.pdf)
- Sarmento & Horta (2020), as summarized by Sun: PCA + OPTICS-clustered pairs on 208 commodity-linked ETFs, 5-minute bars 2009-2018, with costs. Average portfolio Sharpe is 3.79, against 3.58 for the best conventional method. A forecasting overlay cuts drawdown days by about 75% at some cost to profit — [Sun 2025](https://www.wne.uw.edu.pl/application/files/8917/5759/3293/WNE_WP484.pdf)
- Yang & Chen (South African J. Business Management, September 2025), "Pairs trading of nearly identical twin stocks: the case of GOOGL versus GOOG," daily DataStream data 2015-2024. The log spread is modeled as an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with Bertram-style optimal thresholds. Expected returns are 8.71% to 8.36% a year (Sharpe 5.64 to 5.36) as round-trip costs rise from 0.1% to 0.7%, with optimal entry levels of −3.4% to −6.6% log spread. Robustness is checked on RDS.A/RDS.B. These are model-implied expected values from fitted OU parameters, not realized walk-forward P&L — [Yang & Chen 2025](https://www.scielo.org.za/pdf/sajbm/v56n1/29.pdf)
- The GOOGL voting premium was about 1.7% in December 2015. A separate 2015 post says that since the 2014 split the voting shares had ranged up to about 5% above the non-voting shares, with a minimum premium around 0.5% (blog sources) — [Moneyness 2015](https://www.moneyness.ca/2015/12/riding-on-sergey-brin-and-larry-pages.html); [nexus-codex 2015](https://nexus-codex.blogspot.com/2015/12/riding-on-coattails-of-sergey-brin-and.html)

### Inferences
- SPY/IVV/VOO-type arbitrage needs sub-minute execution and full-depth quotes. With IEX-only minute bars and paper fills at NBBO, Frozen_stock cannot measure or capture it honestly. Skip it.
- GOOGL/GOOG is a cheap, near-zero-beta test of the plumbing: two-leg order sync, short-leg handling, and cost measurement. Its edge, if any, is a slow-moving voting-premium spread that can trend for months, for example around buyback mix or index events. Treat any positive result as small capacity and low evidence rate (one pair).
- BRK-A/BRK-B is impractical at about $100k equity, because one BRK-A share costs several hundred thousand dollars (well known, not sourced in this pass). It also converts only A to B.
- Stock versus sector ETF is the natural fit for Frozen_stock. When the stock is cheap, the short leg is the ETF (deeply liquid, ETB); when it is rich, the short leg is a large cap (almost always ETB, see section 5). Hedges across many positions in the same sector net inside the ETF leg, which cuts turnover cost.

### Gaps
- I found no post-2015 US study of single-stock versus sector-SPDR residual reversal net of retail costs.
- I found no rigorous measured history of the GOOGL/GOOG spread (distribution, half-life) after 2016 apart from Yang & Chen's fitted parameters.

## 4. When pairs work: volatility regimes, dislocations, liquidity provision

### Takeaway
Pairs and residual stat-arb earn most of their money in high-volatility dislocations (2000-02, 2008-09, and per Fil, bear markets through 2020) and little or nothing in calm bull markets. The economic source is short-term liquidity provision. Divergences caused by one firm's own news tend not to converge, while divergences from common or liquidity shocks do. Strategies are also exposed to crowded deleveraging episodes (August 2007).

### Cited Findings
- All three Rad, Low & Faff strategies perform better in periods of significant volatility, and cointegration is best in turbulent markets. In "crisis" years (the worst S&P 500 quintile) the cointegration Sortino rises from 0.63 to 0.84. The distance-method Sortino dips from 0.69 to 0.64 — [Rad, Low & Faff 2016](https://pure.bond.edu.au/ws/portalfiles/portal/36339487/AM_The_profitability_of_pairs_trading_strategies.pdf)
- Krauss, Do & Huck: all long-short variants "perform particularly well at such times of high market turmoil." The ensemble earns more than 400% annualized after costs in 09/08-12/09, and October 2008 alone exceeds 100%. They cite Do & Faff (2010), Bogomolov (2013) and Huck (2015) on liquidity-providing pairs strategies faring well in the crisis — [Krauss, Do & Huck working paper](https://www.iwf.rw.fau.de/files/2016/03/03-2016.pdf)
- Do & Faff (2010), per Rad, Low & Faff: the 2007-2009 outperformance came from more pairs completing multiple round trips, and the 2000-2002 outperformance from higher profit per multi-round-trip pair — [Rad, Low & Faff 2016](https://pure.bond.edu.au/ws/portalfiles/portal/36339487/AM_The_profitability_of_pairs_trading_strategies.pdf)
- Engelberg, Gao & Jagannathan ("An Anatomy of Pairs Trading"): profits are lower when the divergence relates to news specific to one stock, and when institutions hold, or analysts cover, both stocks. Closing positions that have not converged within 10 days gives higher risk-adjusted returns (ignoring costs) than holding for 6 months. Profits reflect short-term liquidity provision — [SSRN abstract](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=1330689)
- A secondary summary reports that average pair profits decay from about 0.23% on day 1 to 0.13% on day 2 and below 0.10% a day after day 4 (unverified against the paper) — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=1992)
- Rad, Low & Faff: the liquidity factor is negatively correlated with all strategies' returns, and momentum with the distance and cointegration methods' returns — [Rad, Low & Faff 2016](https://pure.bond.edu.au/ws/portalfiles/portal/36339487/AM_The_profitability_of_pairs_trading_strategies.pdf)
- GOZ: the friction-aware model is more active in 2007-2009 and less active in the calm 2011-2015 bull market. The CNN+Transformer was "nearly completely immune" to the August 2007 quant quake and the 2011-2012 quant losses, which implies simpler strategies were hurt then — [GOZ (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.04028)
- Zhu: pairs trading profit rises with the market risk premium, consistent with arbitrageurs being paid to enforce the Law of One Price — [Zhu 2024](https://economics.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2024-05/Zhu_Pairs_Trading.pdf)

### Inferences
- This is the opposite payoff timing to Frozen_stock's long-only book, which loses in market drawdowns. A pairs lane is valuable less for its average return than as a crisis diversifier, but its evidence will arrive in bursts (2020-type episodes), not steadily.
- The design should screen out single-name news divergences (earnings, guidance, M&A, analyst actions). Frozen_stock already ingests Alpaca news and has an earnings calendar. It should also use a short time stop (about 10 trading days) in line with Engelberg et al.
- The existing ADX/VIX regime tooling can be reused as a diagnostic, not a gate. Record pair P&L by VIX bucket. Do not condition entries on VIX until there is out-of-sample evidence, since the edge is expected to be concentrated in high VIX.

### Gaps
- I found no published pairs-trading results specifically for the 2020 Covid crash or the 2022 bear market in US large caps. Fil (2020) mentions Covid in scope but its abstract gives no numbers.

## 5. Costs and frictions at retail (Alpaca): spread on two legs, borrow, margin, Reg T, paper vs live, Rule 201

### Takeaway
For S&P 500 names and major ETFs, frictions at Alpaca are manageable on paper. ETB shorts carry $0 borrow and locate fees, Reg T gives 2x overnight buying power, and margin interest bites only on a net debit balance. The dominant costs are spread and slippage on four fills per pair round trip. Paper trading understates live costs in three specific ways: no borrow fees, no dividends (including the dividends a short owes), and fills at NBBO with no impact or queue modeling. Rule 201 rarely binds for pairs but can block short entries on crash days.

### Cited Findings
- Alpaca: margin and short selling require at least $2,000 equity. More than 5,000 securities are easy-to-borrow (ETB), and Trading API users pay $0 locate and borrow fees on ETB shares. Borrow status is read from `GET /v2/assets/{symbol}` (`easy_to_borrow`) — [Alpaca margin & short selling docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/margin-and-short-selling)
- Alpaca hard-to-borrow (HTB) rules: an approved locate is required, in 100-share round lots. Locates are single-use (covering does not restore them), and locate fees are not refunded if unused. The daily HTB fee is short market value × HTB rate ÷ 360, charged in the nearest 100-share round lot, with 3 days charged over a weekend — [Alpaca docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/margin-and-short-selling)
- Alpaca short maintenance: for price at or above $5, the greater of $5.00 a share or 30% of market value. For price below $5, the greater of $2.50 a share or 100%. Reg T initial margin is 50% for marginable securities. Overnight buying power is 2x, and intraday buying power up to 4x — [Alpaca docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/margin-and-short-selling)
- Alpaca margin interest: 5.00% (elite) and 6.50% (non-elite) annual, charged only on the end-of-day settlement-date debit balance (rate ÷ 360, 3 days over a weekend). Alpaca may liquidate without notice on a margin shortfall and may change house requirements at any time — [Alpaca docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/margin-and-short-selling)
- Alpaca paper trading: orders fill only when marketable, against NBBO. Order size is not checked against NBBO quantity. Random partial fills occur 10% of the time. There is no modeling of market impact, information leakage, latency slippage or queue position. Borrow fees are "Coming Soon" (not simulated). Dividends and regulatory fees are not simulated — [Alpaca paper trading docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/paper-trading)
- Rule 201 (alternative uptick rule; adopted February 2010, effective May 10, 2010): when a stock falls 10% or more from the prior close, short sales are allowed only above the national best bid for the rest of that day and the next day. It can re-trigger without limit — [SEC press release 2010-26](https://www.sec.gov/news/press/2010/2010-26.htm); [SEC Rule 201 FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/divisions/marketreg/rule201faq.htm)
- Borrow costs: D'Avolio (2002) found about 91% of lent stocks were general collateral, with a value-weighted mean fee of 17 bps, and S&P 500 constituents were "almost always general collateral." Specials (9% of stocks) averaged 4.3% a year — [D'Avolio 2002, JFE](https://www.johnhcochrane.com/s/davolio_borrowing_stock_JF.pdf)
- A 2025 NBER paper (Daniel, Klos & Rottke) puts the typical borrow fee for most stocks at about 25 bps a year (the GC rate). It reports that far more securities are hard-to-borrow today than in D'Avolio's era — [NBER slides](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f228879/f228879.slides.pdf); [NBER paper](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f228879.pdf)
- Institutional pairs backtests charged about 9 bps commission plus 20 bps market impact per stock round trip, doubled for two legs (about 58 bps per complete pair trade) — [Rad, Low & Faff 2016](https://pure.bond.edu.au/ws/portalfiles/portal/36339487/AM_The_profitability_of_pairs_trading_strategies.pdf). GOZ charged 5 bps per unit turnover plus 1 bp a day on shorts — [GOZ (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.04028)

### Inferences
- Margin interest: a dollar-neutral book at about 1x gross (for example $50k long and $50k short on $100k equity) should leave no net debit, since cash plus short proceeds cover the long. Interest then appears only if gross exposure pushes the long side past cash. Keep the lane's gross exposure inside its cash allocation, and log Alpaca's daily interest postings to confirm.
- Dividends are the largest paper-versus-live gap for a short lane. A live short pays the dividend; Alpaca paper simulates none. In pairs, long and short dividends partly offset, but a stock-versus-ETF or high-yield-versus-low-yield pair has a net dividend exposure. The backtest and the paper ledger should both add a modeled payment for each short leg's ex-dividend date (yfinance has dividend history).
- Borrow: restrict the universe to `easy_to_borrow` names and re-check status at entry (it can change). Charge a 25 bps a year GC haircut in the backtest as a conservative proxy, although Alpaca says ETB is $0 to the API user.
- Rule 201 mostly affects the leg that crashed. Pairs short the relative outperformer, so it rarely binds, except on market-wide crash days when many names trip at once. Handle it explicitly: if the short leg is SSR-restricted, skip the entry, because a short-sale limit above the bid may not fill and leaves a naked long. Never leg in one side only.
- Per pair round trip there are four fills: two entries and two exits. At an assumed 1-3 bps half-spread plus slippage per fill in mega caps (assumption, to be measured from Frozen_stock's own fills), cost is about 4-12 bps per pair round trip, plus SEC and FINRA fees on sells. Against a target mean gain of perhaps 30-100 bps per converged trade, costs eat a large share. Hence the emphasis on wide entry bands and low turnover.

### Gaps
- I did not confirm whether Alpaca pays any rebate or interest on short-sale proceeds, whether Alpaca allows fractional-share shorts, or how Alpaca's API flags SSR-restricted symbols. The Alpaca pages read did not cover SSR or PDT.
- I did not source current quoted spreads for S&P 500 names or current SEC Section 31 and FINRA TAF rates.
- I did not research FINRA's pattern-day-trader rule changes in 2025-2026. They are likely irrelevant at about $100k equity, but should be checked if the lane's trades go same-day.

## 6. Expected evidence rate and correlation between pair returns

### Takeaway
A GGR-style book trades roughly 20-25 distinct pairs a month (about 5-6 new trades a week). About 60% of trades converge, with a median hold of about 14 trading days; the other 40% end as losses. Per-trade outcomes are noisy (standard deviation about 6%), so confirming a thin post-2015 edge needs hundreds of closed trades. Pair returns show no correlation with the market, but they are correlated with each other through shared legs, sectors, and liquidity or deleveraging shocks.

### Cited Findings
- Rad, Low & Faff, 1962-2014, distance method: 4,061 distinct pairs traded, with a sum of distinct pairs per month of 15,087 over the sample. 62.53% of trades converged (mean +4.26%, sd 2.38%, mean 21.15 days open, median 14) and 37.47% did not (mean −3.99%, sd 7.02%). Cointegration is similar (61.35% converged, median 15 days). Copula converges on only 39.98% — [Rad, Low & Faff 2016](https://pure.bond.edu.au/ws/portalfiles/portal/36339487/AM_The_profitability_of_pairs_trading_strategies.pdf)
- Raising the opening threshold lowers trades per pair per 6-month period and lengthens convergence time (Rad, Low & Faff sensitivity analysis) — [Rad, Low & Faff 2016](https://pure.bond.edu.au/ws/portalfiles/portal/36339487/AM_The_profitability_of_pairs_trading_strategies.pdf)
- Pair-strategy returns show no evidence of correlation with market excess returns, but are negatively related to the liquidity factor (and momentum for the distance and cointegration methods) — [Rad, Low & Faff 2016](https://pure.bond.edu.au/ws/portalfiles/portal/36339487/AM_The_profitability_of_pairs_trading_strategies.pdf)
- From 2009, distance and cointegration trading opportunities fell considerably — [Rad, Low & Faff 2016](https://pure.bond.edu.au/ws/portalfiles/portal/36339487/AM_The_profitability_of_pairs_trading_strategies.pdf)
- Avellaneda & Lee tie the August 2007 stat-arb drawdown to simultaneous unwinding by quant funds, a common shock across many residual positions — [Avellaneda & Lee 2010](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v10y2010i7p761-782.html)

### Inferences
- Arithmetic from Rad, Low & Faff's trade mix (my calculation): per-trade mean = 0.625×4.26 + 0.375×(−3.99) ≈ 1.17%, and per-trade sd ≈ 6.2%. A t-stat of 2 then needs about (2×6.2/1.17)² ≈ 110 trades at the full-sample edge. At a post-2009 edge nearer 0.3-0.4% per trade, it needs about 1,000-1,700 trades. At 5-6 trades a week, that is roughly 3.5-6 years, or far more trades per week than a top-20-pairs book produces.
- The rate can be raised with a larger set of stock-versus-sector-ETF residual signals (about 500 stocks, each eligible) and with intraday-to-few-day holds, at the cost of more turnover. GOZ's finding that about half the Sharpe survives a one-week hold suggests holds of 3-10 days.
- Correlation: pair returns should be far less correlated with each other than Frozen_stock's long-only trades, which share market beta. Cluster risk remains: pairs that share a leg, the same sector ETF hedge, or exposure to a market-wide liquidity shock. Pre-register a cap such as one open pair per stock leg and at most N pairs per sector. Measure the realized average pairwise correlation of daily pair P&L, and compare its effective number of independent bets with the long-only book.
- Because the edge is concentrated in volatile periods, a calm-period paper trial may show near-zero returns even if the strategy is sound. This argues for a backtest-first gate with explicit crisis sub-periods (2015-16, 2018 Q4, 2020, 2022, 2025) rather than a short paper test alone.

### Gaps
- I found no published post-2015 counts of trades per week or average pairwise correlation of pair returns for S&P 500-only books. The figures above come from 1962-2014 all-market data plus my arithmetic.

## 7. Recommendation: build a market-neutral lane?, which method, and the pre-registered rule

### Takeaway
Build a backtest first, and a small paper lane only if it passes. The literature supports a thin but real residual-reversal edge in large caps that is concentrated in volatile markets and highly sensitive to costs. Frozen_stock's advantages are near-zero commissions, mega-cap spreads, $0 ETB borrow, and the diversification value against a high-beta long book. The best-fitting design is stock versus sector-ETF residual mean reversion (Avellaneda-Lee ETF method) with turnover control and a news and earnings filter. Run GGR or cointegration within-sector pairs as the benchmark, and GOOGL/GOOG as a plumbing and cost-calibration pair. Judge it by a pre-registered after-cost, beta-neutral rule with a minimum trade count.

### Cited Findings
- The sector-ETF residual approach earned Sharpe 1.1-1.51 after costs in 1997-2007 and is "stable during structural shifts" in a 2005-2024 non-US replication — [Avellaneda & Lee 2010](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v10y2010i7p761-782.html); [Sun 2025](https://www.wne.uw.edu.pl/application/files/8917/5759/3293/WNE_WP484.pdf)
- Naive daily full-turnover stat-arb in the S&P 500 lost 14-25% a year after 5 bps half-turn costs in 2010-2015, despite positive gross returns — [Krauss, Do & Huck](https://www.iwf.rw.fau.de/files/2016/03/03-2016.pdf)
- A cost-aware objective keeps net Sharpe about 1 in roughly the S&P 500 universe through 2016 — [GOZ (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.04028)
- Non-converging divergences driven by single-stock news, and positions open longer than about 10 days, are where losses concentrate — [Engelberg, Gao & Jagannathan (SSRN)](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=1330689); [Rad, Low & Faff 2016](https://pure.bond.edu.au/ws/portalfiles/portal/36339487/AM_The_profitability_of_pairs_trading_strategies.pdf)
- Paper trading does not simulate borrow fees or dividends and fills at NBBO without impact — [Alpaca paper trading docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/paper-trading)

### Inferences
Candidate designs to backtest, in priority order:
1. **Stock versus sector-SPDR residual reversal (primary).** Universe: current S&P 500 members that are `easy_to_borrow`, each hedged against its SPDR sector ETF (XLK, XLF, XLV, ...), with an optional SPY second factor. Fit a rolling 60-day regression of daily returns on the ETF, and fit an OU model to the cumulative residual to get an s-score (Avellaneda-Lee style). Enter at |s| ≥ 2, or a pre-registered threshold. Exit at |s| ≤ 0.5, or at a 10-trading-day time stop, or at a residual stop. Skip entries within ±2 days of earnings or on a same-day single-name news hit (reuse the news pipeline). Size beta-neutral with daily dollar rebalancing of the ETF hedge only, netted across positions in the same sector. Cap open signals at 1 per stock, and set a per-sector cap.
2. **Within-GICS-sector distance or cointegration pairs (benchmark).** 12-month formation and 6-month trading, top 20-50 pairs by SSD or Engle-Granger p-value, open at 2σ, close at convergence, with a 10-day non-convergence stop. This is the literature-comparable baseline. It will likely show a lower trade rate after 2009, as Rad, Low & Faff found.
3. **GOOGL/GOOG spread (plumbing and cost calibration).** Daily z-score of the log spread, with minute-bar execution through IEX. Its main value is measuring real two-leg slippage and SSR or borrow handling with near-zero market risk.
- Skip: SPY/IVV-type ETF arbitrage (needs sub-minute execution), BRK-A/B (share price), copula methods (low convergence rate, worst after costs in Rad, Low & Faff), and deep-learning stat-arb (gross Sharpe is impressive, but the net edge needs institutional-grade cost modeling and many parameters, a poor fit for a small auditable bot).

Backtest hygiene specific to this lane:
- yfinance lacks delisted names. A current-constituent S&P 500 universe drops merged and acquired firms, whose pairs may have diverged permanently. That is the same survivorship problem that blocked PEAD in Frozen_stock. Mitigations: use sector-ETF hedging, so the hedge leg never delists; run a point-in-time membership check where possible; and treat results as an upper bound.
- Model live costs explicitly: measured half-spread on four fills, SEC and FINRA fees on sells, short-leg dividends paid, a 25 bps a year GC borrow haircut, and margin interest only if gross exceeds the cash allocation.

Proposed pre-registered rule (state it before any results are seen):
- Stage A, the backtest gate (2015-01 to latest, walk-forward, parameters frozen from 2005-2014 data). Pass only if all of these hold after modeled costs:
  - (i) The mean net return per closed signal is above 0 with t ≥ 2 (Newey-West on daily lane P&L).
  - (ii) The annualized net Sharpe of the lane is at least 0.5.
  - (iii) |β| to SPY is at most 0.1, and the correlation of daily P&L with the long-only book is at most 0.3.
  - (iv) The result is positive in at least 2 of the 3 non-crisis sub-periods (it must not be all 2020).
  - (v) At least 300 closed signals.
- Stage B, the paper lane (small notional, for example ≤10% of equity gross). Run until at least 150 closed pair trades and at least 6 months. Pass if the net mean per trade, after adding modeled dividends and borrow to Alpaca's paper P&L, is above 0 and within the Stage A 90% confidence interval, and if realized slippage per fill is at or below the backtest assumption. Fail and stop if realized costs exceed the assumption by more than 50% or if the lane's drawdown exceeds 2× the backtest maximum.
- Report a separate "crisis bucket" (VIX above 25) every time, because the evidence says most of the edge sits there.

### Gaps
- The design thresholds (|s| ≥ 2, 60-day window, 10-day stop, 0.5 Sharpe bar, 300 and 150 trade counts) are reasoned choices from the cited work and the power arithmetic above, not values validated for 2015-2026 US large caps.
- I did not verify Avellaneda & Lee's exact published s-score thresholds in the primary text.
